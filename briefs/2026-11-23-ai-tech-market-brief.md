## 5. Foundations & Terms

### Concept Spotlight: All-Stock Deal

**What it is:** An all-stock deal is a merger or acquisition where the buyer pays the seller's shareholders using its own shares (stock) instead of cash.

**Why it exists:** It allows the acquirer to preserve cash for operations or other investments. It is also tax-efficient for the seller's shareholders (they defer capital gains taxes until they sell the new shares). It aligns the interests of both sets of shareholders in the combined company's future success.

**A worked example with real numbers:** CrowdStrike (CRWD) is buying Zscaler (ZS) for roughly $44 billion in an all-stock deal. This means Zscaler shareholders will receive a fixed number of CrowdStrike shares for each Zscaler share they own. The exact "exchange ratio" determines the value. If the ratio is 0.5, each ZS share turns into 0.5 CRWD shares. If CRWD stock goes up before the deal closes, ZS shareholders get more value. If CRWD stock goes down, they get less. This is different from a cash deal, where the price is fixed regardless of the acquirer's stock price.

**How it shows up in the news:** "CrowdStrike to acquire Zscaler in an all-stock transaction valued at $43.9 billion."

**A figure worth remembering:** The exchange ratio. It is the single most important number in an all-stock deal because it determines the final value for the seller's shareholders.

**Common misconception:** That an all-stock deal is "free" for the acquirer. It is not. It dilutes (reduces the ownership percentage of) existing CrowdStrike shareholders. They now own a piece of a bigger company, but their proportional claim on earnings is lower than before the deal. The acquirer's shareholders are paying with their own future earnings.

**Other Terms from Today's News:**

- **Looped Transformer (Recurrent Depth):** An AI model architecture that runs the same transformer block multiple times, sharing the same weights (parameters) across each loop. This gives the model more "thinking steps" (depth) without adding new parameters. It is like reading a sentence twice to understand it better, using the same brain. GLM-5.4 uses this.

- **Adversarial Distillation:** A technique where a smaller, cheaper AI model is trained to mimic the behavior of a larger, more powerful "frontier" model by querying it and learning from its outputs. OpenAI calls this an "existential threat" because it allows competitors to steal the capabilities of expensive closed models without authorization (Source 36).

- **Sell-sidecar (Circuit Breaker):** A mechanism on a stock exchange (like South Korea's KOSPI) that temporarily halts trading in index futures when they fall too sharply in a short period. It is designed to prevent panic selling and give the market time to find a stable price. The KOSPI triggered its 32nd such circuit breaker recently (Source 38).

- **War-risk premium:** The extra cost added to the price of oil (or insurance for ships) to account for the risk of conflict in a producing or transit region. The tanker attacks in the Gulf are injecting a war-risk premium into global oil prices right now.
