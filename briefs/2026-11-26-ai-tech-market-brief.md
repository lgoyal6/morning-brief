## 5. Foundations & Terms

### Concept Spotlight: Key Person Risk (or Key Man Risk)
**What it is in plain words:** The risk that a company's value drops significantly if a single person—usually the founder, CEO, or top scientist—leaves, dies, or becomes unable to work.

**Why it exists:** Many companies, especially in tech, are built entirely around the vision, talent, and relationships of one person. Succession plans are often weak. Investors and partners trust *that person*, not just the company.

**A concrete worked example with real numbers:** S&P Global is considering downgrading Nvidia's credit rating from AA to AA- because of key person risk tied to CEO Jensen Huang ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/sp-considers-nvidia-credit-downgrade-over-governance-and-key-man-risks/)). A downgrade raises Nvidia's cost of borrowing. If Nvidia has $10 billion in debt, a 0.25% higher interest rate costs them an extra $25 million per year. More importantly, it signals to the market that the company is riskier, which can depress the stock price.

**How it shows up in the news:** Credit rating actions ("S&P considers Nvidia downgrade over key man risks"), stock drops on CEO health news, governance reports criticizing lack of succession planning.

**A figure worth remembering:** Nvidia's stock fell ~20% on the day of the DeepSeek scare, partly because the market panicked about Jensen Huang's leadership being challenged. The S&P downgrade consideration makes this risk official.

**A common misconception:** Key person risk is not just about the CEO dying. It's about the loss of *specific, irreplaceable talent*. For Nvidia, it's Jensen's ability to set the technical roadmap. For OpenAI, it was Ilya Sutskever. For Tesla, it's Elon Musk.

- **Credit Rating Downgrade:** A reduction in a company's credit rating (e.g., from AA to AA-). It signals to lenders that the borrower is riskier, usually raising the company's cost of borrowing. S&P is considering this for Nvidia due to governance and key man risks.
