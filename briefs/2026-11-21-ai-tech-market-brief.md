## 5. Foundations & Terms

### Concept Spotlight: Correction (Bull Market, Bear Market, Correction)

**What it is in plain words:** A "correction" is a decline of 10% or more in a stock market index (like the S&P 500) from its most recent peak. It is the market's way of "correcting" itself after a period of excessive optimism.

**Why it exists:** Markets don't go up in a straight line. Prices overshoot on the way up (driven by euphoria, FOMO, momentum) and on the way down (driven by fear, margin calls, forced selling). A correction is the natural mechanism that resets valuations to more reasonable levels.

**A concrete worked example with real numbers:** The S&P 500 hit a record high of 5,800 on September 15, 2026. It closed at 5,200 on November 20, 2026 — a decline of 10.3%. That is a correction. It does not mean the bull market is over. It means the market has given back some of its gains.

**How it shows up in the news:** "S&P 500 enters correction territory" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/sp-500-correction-market-sell-off.html)). The media will use dramatic language, but corrections are common. Since 1950, the S&P 500 has experienced a correction roughly every 1-2 years.

**A figure worth remembering:** About 1 in 3 corrections turns into a bear market (a decline of 20% or more). That means 2 out of 3 corrections are buying opportunities for long-term investors.

**A common misconception:** A correction is not a signal to sell everything. In fact, trying to time corrections is a losing strategy. The best approach is to stay invested, rebalance if needed, and wait for the recovery. The average correction lasts about 4 months and is followed by a new high within 12 months.

**Terms from today's news:**

- **War-risk insurance premium:** An extra charge that shipping companies pay to insure their vessels when they transit through dangerous waters (like the Strait of Hormuz). When the risk of attack rises, the premium jumps. This cost gets passed on to the price of the oil being transported. In today's news, the tanker attacks will cause war-risk premiums to spike, making every barrel of oil passing through the Strait more expensive.

- **Looped transformer (recurrent depth):** A type of AI model architecture where the same set of neural network layers is applied multiple times in sequence, like a loop. This gives the model more "thinking time" per token without adding more parameters. Inkling 2 Mini uses this — it gets the benefit of a deeper model (more computation) while keeping the memory footprint of a smaller model. The trade-off is slower inference (more computation per token).

- **Proof assistant (Lean):** A software tool that helps mathematicians write and verify proofs. Lean is a programming language where every step of a proof must be specified in a way the computer can check. The Riemann Hypothesis claim was released as 17 million lines of Lean code — meaning every logical step is machine-verifiable, which is both a strength (no hidden assumptions) and a weakness (impossible for humans to read).
