## 5. Foundations & Terms

### Concept Spotlight: Section 301 Tariffs

**What it is in plain words:** A tool in US trade law that lets the president slap tariffs (taxes on imports) on countries that the US government decides are cheating on trade.

**Why it exists:** To protect American industries from foreign practices like stealing intellectual property, forcing technology transfers, or flooding markets with subsidized goods.

**A concrete worked example with real numbers:** The Trump administration just used Section 301 to propose an extra 20% tariff on Chinese goods. If a US company imports $100 worth of Chinese electronics, they now have to pay the US government an extra $20. That cost is usually passed on to the American consumer, making the electronics $120.

**How it shows up in the news today:** "Trump announces new Section 301 tariffs on 14 economies, including China and the EU" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/us-tariffs-trump-section-301.html)).

**A figure worth remembering:** Section 301 was famously used in 2018 to impose tariffs on over $300 billion worth of Chinese goods, kicking off the first phase of the US-China trade war.

**A common misconception:** That the exporting country (China, the EU) pays the tariff. The US importer pays the tariff to the US government. The foreign exporter may lower their price to stay competitive, but the direct payment is made by the US buyer.

**Terms from today's news:**

- **All-cash deal:** A merger or acquisition where the buyer pays entirely in cash, not its own stock. Sanofi's $41B bid for Insmed is an all-cash deal. This gives certainty to Insmed's shareholders (they get cash, not risky Sanofi stock) and signals strong conviction from Sanofi.

- **Backlog:** The total value of confirmed customer orders that a company has received but not yet delivered or recognized as revenue. Dell reported a strong backlog, but noted it is "rigorously vetted for customer quality and access to capital" ([MarketWatch](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/dells-stock-rises-as-the-ai-boom-helps-sales-more-than-double-cfb8cf65?mod=mw_rss_topstories)). A backlog is future revenue, but only if the customer can actually pay.

- **Sovereign AI:** The movement by nations to build their own AI infrastructure (data centers, models, talent) to ensure data sovereignty, national security, and economic independence from US and Chinese tech giants. Aleph Alpha's Kolibri 1.1 is a prime example of a Sovereign AI model for Europe.

- **Key-person risk:** The risk that a company's performance depends heavily on a single individual (founder, CEO, star scientist). Nvidia's Jensen Huang is the textbook definition. His resignation (and reinstatement) caused the stock to swing wildly. The lack of a succession plan amplifies this risk.
