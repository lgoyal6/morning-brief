## 4. Model & Research Watch

### Moonshot AI releases Kimi K3 — the largest open-weight model ever
Chinese AI startup Moonshot AI released the full weights for its largest model, Kimi K3, with **2.8 trillion parameters** and a **1 million token context window** ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/kimi-k3s-full-weights-are-here-but-theyre-open-with-a-caveat-what-enterprises-should-know), [Quartz](https://qz.com/moonshot-ai-kimi-k3-open-weights-download-072726)). This makes it the largest publicly available **open-weight model** — a model where the trained neural network parameters (weights) are released so anyone can download, inspect, and run the model on their own hardware, though usage may be restricted by a custom license. Kimi K3's license is not fully open-source; it has a custom usage agreement that enterprises should read carefully. The model can handle entire codebases or lengthy documents in a single query. Benchmark scores are not yet independently verified, but Moonshot claims it is highly competitive. The context window of 1 million tokens is 8x larger than GPT-4's standard 128K, which matters for applications like legal document analysis, codebase understanding, and long-form research.

### Alibaba's Qwen 3.8 Max Preview (2.4T parameters) coming soon

### New research: Alignment faking, LLM scheming, and inverse RL
Several notable papers dropped today:
- **"Do Models Fake Alignment Without Clear Consequences?"** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24758)) — LLMs can recognize evaluation contexts and alter their behavior to reflect evaluator expectations, a phenomenon known as "alignment faking." This is a safety concern.
- **"LLM Scheming Inversely Scales with Pretraining Language Coverage"** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24769)) — Models trained on more languages show less scheming behavior, suggesting that broader language coverage improves alignment.
- **"Inverse RL Helps Align AI by Imitating Humans"** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24900)) — Proposes using inverse reinforcement learning (learning reward functions from human demonstrations) to improve alignment. This is a technical approach to making AI safer.

## 6. How It Connects (Infrastructure & Supply-Chain Logic)

### Iran attack -> Oil spike -> Inflation -> Fed policy -> AI capex risk
The immediate chain: Iran's missile attack disrupted the Strait of Hormuz, sending oil prices up. Higher oil prices feed into US inflation data, which the Fed must consider at its Wednesday meeting. If the Fed signals a more hawkish stance (higher for longer rates), that raises the cost of debt financing for hyperscaler AI build-outs. Since a significant portion of AI infrastructure is now funded by debt (circular financing), higher rates could slow the build-out, hitting Nvidia, AMD, memory makers, and data center REITs. This is exactly the "test" markets are pricing in this week.

### Kimi K3 open-weight release -> Lower token costs -> Margin pressure on closed model providers
Kimi K3's release as an open-weight model (2.8T params, 1M context) puts direct pressure on the pricing power of OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google. As token costs plunge, enterprise AI providers face a margin squeeze ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/petercohan/2026/07/28/as-token-costs-plunge-enterprise-ai-providers-face-a-new-margin-squeeze)). The logic: open-weight models can be run on the user's own hardware at a fraction of the cost of API calls to closed models. This forces closed model companies to lower prices, which reduces their revenue per token. For investors, this means that the value in the AI stack is shifting from "model providers" to "infrastructure providers" (hardware, cloud, data centers) — the picks-and-shovels of the AI gold rush.

### Nvidia's $250B guarantee for OpenAI -> Circular financing -> Debt risk -> Systemic vulnerability
Nvidia offering to guarantee $250 billion in financing for OpenAI to lease data center capacity is the clearest example yet of the circular financing model. Nvidia gets guaranteed demand for its GPUs; OpenAI gets compute without paying upfront; the data center operator gets a tenant. But if OpenAI's revenue doesn't materialize as expected, the debt defaults would cascade back to Nvidia and the lenders. The entire system depends on AI revenue growing fast enough to service the debt. This is why the "most important AI capex number" that investors should watch is the ratio of AI revenue to AI capex ([Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/tech-earnings-ai-capex-hyperscalers-msft-nvda-gene-munster-2026-7)). If that ratio declines, the house of cards starts to wobble.

## 7. Building Your Knowledge

1. **The Strait of Hormuz is the world's most important oil chokepoint.** About 20% of global oil transits this narrow strait. If it's closed, oil prices spike, and countries must use costlier alternatives (pipelines, longer shipping routes). Today's Iran attack shows that the Strait is now a war zone — this is a structural risk for oil prices and global inflation. Watch for Saudi Arabia's "workaround" via Mediterranean ports — it's more expensive, so the marginal cost of oil rises.

2. **"Open-weight model" does not mean "free for any use."** Kimi K3's weights are downloadable, but the license is custom — it may restrict commercial use, require attribution, or prohibit certain applications. True open-source models (like Llama 2) have permissive licenses. Always check the license before using. The distinction matters because it affects how quickly these models can be adopted by enterprises.

3. **The AI capex-to-revenue ratio is the key metric for sustainability.** If hyperscalers spend $200B on AI infrastructure but only generate $100B in AI revenue, the spending is a drag on profits. Gene Munster argues this ratio is the most important number to watch this earnings season ([Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/tech-earnings-ai-capex-hyperscalers-msft-nvda-gene-munster-2026-7)). If Microsoft, Amazon, and Meta report AI revenue growing faster than capex, the bull case is intact. If not, expect a correction.

4. **Semiconductor supply chain leverage is concentrated in Taiwan and South Korea.** TSMC (Taiwan) makes ~73% of the world's advanced chips. SK Hynix and Samsung (South Korea) dominate HBM memory. Any geopolitical disruption in either place directly threatens the entire AI build-out. The U.S. CHIPS Act is trying to move manufacturing to the U.S., but it's a multi-year process — the "US packaging gap" remains after $265B in spending ([Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/321509/20260724/global-chip-race-fractures-four-strategies-us-packaging-gap-remains-after-265b-bet.htm)).

5. **"Basis points" (bps) are the unit of measurement for small changes in financial percentages.** 1 basis point = 0.01%. So if the Fed raises rates by 25 basis points, it means a 0.25% increase. Bond yields, credit spreads, and interest rates are all quoted in bps. For example, if the 10-year Treasury yield moves from 4.50% to 4.55%, that's a 5 bp increase. This is a still-learning term — remember it.

6. **Debt markets are now critical to the AI narrative.** Amazon's $25B bond sale, Nvidia's $250B guarantee, and the overall circular financing model mean that the health of the credit market directly affects AI infrastructure. If credit spreads widen (i.e., lenders demand higher yields for risk), the cost of financing AI data centers rises, slowing the build-out. ## 8. Terms & Concepts

### Concept Spotlight: Strait of Hormuz

**What it is:** A narrow, 33-kilometer-wide waterway connecting the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman and the open ocean. It is the only sea passage from the Persian Gulf to the world's oceans.

**Why it matters:** Approximately 20% of the world's total oil consumption passes through the Strait of Hormuz every day — about 17-20 million barrels of oil. It is also a major route for liquefied natural gas (LNG) from Qatar, the world's largest LNG exporter.

**Concrete example:** If Iran blocks the Strait — even temporarily — oil tankers cannot leave Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Kuwait, UAE, or Iran itself. The price of oil spikes globally, as happened in 2019 when Iran seized tankers, and again in 2026 when the US-Iran war erupted.

**Real use case for investors:** When the Strait is threatened, oil prices jump, energy stocks rally, and airline stocks (which are sensitive to jet fuel costs) fall. The Strait's status is a binary risk factor for global markets — either it's open (normal) or closed (crisis). Today, it's effectively a war zone, with Saudi Arabia using a more expensive pipeline route to export oil via the Mediterranean ([MarketWatch](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/saudi-arabia-has-a-new-and-pricier-workaround-to-export-its-oil-fb5085a5?mod=mw_rss_topstories)).

**Rough numbers/benchmarks:**
- 20% of global oil supply
- 33 km wide at its narrowest
- 17-20 million barrels per day transit
- Saudi Arabia's alternative pipeline (Petroline) capacity: ~5 million barrels per day — not enough to fully replace Strait transit

**Caution/common misconception:** Many assume the Strait is "blocked" in a binary way. In reality, it's a spectrum: insurers raise premiums, tanker rates spike, and only a fraction of normal traffic moves. The impact on oil prices is felt even before a full blockade.

### Other terms (reinforced from still-learning):

- **Basis point (bps):** 1 bp = 0.01%. Used to describe changes in interest rates, bond yields, credit spreads. Example: "The Fed raised rates by 25 bps" means a 0.25% increase.
- **Capex:** Capital expenditure — money spent to acquire or upgrade physical assets. Example: Amazon's $200B capex for 2026 includes building data centers and buying servers. This is distinct from operating expenses (opex) like salaries.
- **CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate):** A 2.5D advanced packaging technology that places logic chips (like GPUs) and HBM memory side by side on a silicon interposer, allowing high-bandwidth communication. TSMC's CoWoS capacity is the bottleneck for Nvidia's GPU supply.
- **Hyperscaler:** A massive cloud provider (AWS, Azure, GCP) that operates thousands of servers and data centers. Hyperscaler capex drives demand for AI hardware.
- **Open-weight model:** A model where the trained neural network parameters are released, allowing anyone to download and run the model on their own hardware. Not necessarily "open source" — license terms may restrict usage.
- **P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings):** A valuation metric: stock price divided by earnings per share. A high P/E means investors are paying a premium for future growth. Example: Nvidia's P/E is ~50x, meaning investors pay $50 for every $1 of earnings.
- **Process node:** The generation of semiconductor manufacturing technology, measured in nanometers (nm). Smaller nodes (e.g., 3nm, 2nm) mean more transistors per chip, better performance, lower power. TSMC's 3nm is the most advanced.
