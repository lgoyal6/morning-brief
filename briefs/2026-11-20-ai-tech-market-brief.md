## 5. Foundations & Terms

**Concept Spotlight: The Greenshoe (Overallotment Option)**
**What it is:** A greenshoe (formally called an "overallotment option") is a provision in an IPO underwriting agreement that allows the underwriters (the investment banks managing the IPO) to sell more shares to the public than originally planned. It is named after the Green Shoe Manufacturing Company, which first used this provision in 1960.
**Why it exists:** It serves two purposes. First, it provides price stability for the newly issued stock. If the stock price falls below the IPO price, the underwriters can buy back shares in the open market using the greenshoe shares, creating a "floor" for the price. Second, it allows the company to raise more capital if demand is high.
**Concrete worked example with real numbers:** Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share. The initial offering was for ~1 billion shares, raising ~$81 billion. The underwriters had a greenshoe option to sell an additional 15% (~150 million shares). Because demand was strong, they exercised the greenshoe, raising the total haul to $93.5 billion ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/anthropic-ipo-greenshoe.html)).
**How it shows up in the news:** "Underwriters exercised the greenshoe" means the IPO was very popular. If the stock falls below the IPO price (as Anthropic did), the underwriters can use the greenshoe to buy back shares and support the price.
**A figure worth remembering:** The greenshoe is typically 15% of the base offering.
**A caution / common misconception:** The greenshoe does not guarantee the stock price will stay above the IPO price. It is a stabilization mechanism for the first 30 days. Anthropic falling 14% on day two shows the limits of this stabilization.

**Terms from Today's News:**
- **Short Seller Report:** A detailed analysis published by an investment firm that has bet against a company's stock (shorted it). The report argues that the company is overvalued or has hidden risks. The goal is to drive the stock price down so the short seller can profit. AMD was hit by a short seller report alleging uncontrolled China exposure.
- **War-Risk Insurance Premium:** The extra cost that ship owners pay to insure vessels traveling through a dangerous area (like the Strait of Hormuz). When geopolitical tensions rise, these premiums skyrocket, adding to the cost of global trade.
- **Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI):** In AI, the ability of an AI system to improve its own code, architecture, or training process without human intervention. Google's "ScientistTwo" paper is an example. An AI that can run its own research loop is a key step towards AGI.
