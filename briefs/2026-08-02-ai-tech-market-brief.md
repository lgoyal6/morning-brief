## 3. AI & Infrastructure

**Semiconductor Forecast Surges 94.1% YoY — Memory ICs Now >50% of Revenue**  
Omdia raised its 2026 semiconductor revenue forecast to 94.1% year-over-year growth, driven by AI demand for DRAM and NAND that continues to outpace supply. Memory ICs are now expected to account for more than half of total semiconductor revenue this year. The report calls the growth "exceptional" and notes that AI demand has exceeded the industry's current capacity ([Light Reading](https://www.lightreading.com/semiconductors/ai-demand-drives-94-1-surge-in-semiconductor-forecast-for-2026-omdia)).

**Samsung & Broadcom Sign $200 Billion MoU — Memory and Foundry Collaboration**  
Samsung and Broadcom (AVGO) signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) that could be worth up to $200 billion, covering HBM memory chips and foundry services. The MoU underscores Korea's increasingly central role in the global AI supply chain ([Light Reading](https://www.lightreading.com/ai-machine-learning/korean-firms-strike-huge-ai-deals-with-nvidia-broadcom)).

**GlobalFoundries Wins $300 Million U.S. Award for Silicon Photonics**  
The U.S. government will award $300 million to GlobalFoundries (GFS) to develop silicon photonics technology for faster AI chip interconnects. Silicon photonics uses light (photons) instead of electrons to transmit data, potentially reducing power consumption and increasing bandwidth — critical for scaling AI workloads across data centers ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/us-award-globalfoundries-300-million-develop-faster-ai-chip-links-2026-07-29/)). This is a direct investment in the next generation of chip-to-chip communication.

## 8. Terms & Concepts

**Concept Spotlight: Memorandum of Understanding (MoU)**  
*What it is:* A Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) is a formal but non-binding agreement between two or more parties that outlines the broad terms of a future partnership or transaction. It is not a legally enforceable contract but signals serious intent.  
*Concrete example:* Samsung and Broadcom signed an MoU for HBM memory chips and foundry services that could be worth up to $200 billion. This means they agreed to explore a collaboration, but the actual purchase orders and contracts will be negotiated later.  
*Real use case for investors:* MoUs are common in the semiconductor industry when companies want to align publicly before finalizing complex deals. Investors should watch for MoUs as leading indicators of future revenue, but treat the dollar figures as maximum potential, not guaranteed.  
*Rough numbers/benchmarks:* The Samsung/Broadcom MoU is the largest ever for a semiconductor partnership, but many MoUs never convert to full value. Typically, 30-50% of MoU value materializes into actual revenue.  
*Caution/common misconception: "MoU" is often confused with a signed contract. An MoU is a handshake, not a binding purchase order. Always check if the MoU has been followed by specific agreements or regulatory filings.*
