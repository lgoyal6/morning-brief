## 4. Beginner Knowledge Lens

1. **Capex vs. Free Cash Flow:** Capital expenditure (capex) is money spent on long-term assets (data centers, chips). Free cash flow (FCF) is the cash left after capex. If capex grows faster than revenue, FCF shrinks. Investors worry when FCF drops because it limits dividends, buybacks, and debt repayment. Google's rising capex is squeezing its FCF.

2. **Oil Prices and Inflation:** Oil is a key input for many goods (transportation, plastics). When oil prices rise, inflation expectations rise. The Fed then may raise interest rates to cool the economy. Higher rates make stocks (especially growth stocks) less attractive because future earnings are discounted more heavily.

3. **Open-Weight vs. Closed-Weight AI Models:** Open-weight models release the trained parameters (the "weights") publicly. Anyone can use them, modify them, or run them. Closed-weight models (like earlier versions of GPT-4) keep the weights secret. Open models reduce barriers to entry but may also reduce the commercial advantage of AI labs.

4. **Data Center Power Consumption:** A typical hyperscale data center can use 100-200 megawatts (MW) of electricity — enough to power 50,000-100,000 homes. The projected growth to 20% of U.S. electricity by 2035 means every new data center must compete with residential and industrial users for power, driving up costs and leading to local opposition.

5. **HBM (High-Bandwidth Memory):** HBM is a type of DRAM that is stacked vertically and placed close to the GPU. It provides extremely fast data transfer (up to ~1 TB/s in HBM3e). It's essential for AI training because models need to read and write data quickly. The global HBM market is dominated by SK Hynix, Samsung, and Micron.

6. **Technical Triple Threat:** When Treasury yields, oil prices, and the U.S. dollar all rise at the same time, it's historically been a tough environment for stocks. Yields compete with stocks for investor money, oil hurts corporate profits, and a strong dollar reduces the value of overseas earnings for U.S. companies.

## 5. Terms Used Today

1. **Free Cash Flow (FCF):** Cash generated from operations minus capital expenditures. *Example: If a company earns $100B in cash from operations and spends $60B on capex, its FCF is $40B. A declining FCF signals that growth investments are consuming cash.*

2. **HBM (High-Bandwidth Memory):** A 3D-stacked DRAM technology that provides high-speed data transfer for AI GPUs. *Rough benchmark: HBM3e bandwidth ~1 TB/s; typical DDR5 DIMM bandwidth ~0.05 TB/s. HBM is essential for training large models.*

3. **Capex (Capital Expenditure):** Money spent on physical assets like buildings, machinery, or data centers. *Example: Google's $50B+ in AI capex this year is its expenditure on data centers, GPUs, and networking equipment.*

4. **Hyperscaler:** A large cloud provider that operates massive data centers at scale — e.g., Amazon Web Services (AWS), Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud. They are the primary customers of AI hardware.

5. **Open-Weight Model:** An AI model whose trained parameters (weights) are publicly released, allowing anyone to download, run, and modify it. *Example: Meta's Llama models are open-weight; early GPT-4 was not.*

6. **CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate):** A 2.5D packaging technology used by TSMC to stack multiple chips (e.g., GPU + HBM) on a single substrate. It's a bottleneck for AI chip production because capacity is limited.

7. **Shadow Fleet:** Ships used to transport oil or goods in violation of sanctions, often by using opaque ownership, fake flags, or disabling tracking. The EU's new sanctions target these vessels.

8. **Strait of Hormuz:** A narrow waterway between Iran and Oman through which about 20% of the world's oil passes. Any disruption there (e.g., from war) can spike oil prices globally.

9. **P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings):** A valuation metric: stock price divided by earnings per share. *Example: A stock at $100 with $5 EPS has a P/E of 20. A high P/E (e.g., 30+) often implies high growth expectations, which are vulnerable to rate hikes or earnings misses.*

10. **Grid Interconnection:** The process of connecting a new power plant or data center to the electrical grid. It can take years due to permitting and equipment backlogs, pushing up costs ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/business/energy-oil/americas-demand-for-power-is-soaringand-so-are-the-costs-of-building-out-the-grid-f57b5adb)).
