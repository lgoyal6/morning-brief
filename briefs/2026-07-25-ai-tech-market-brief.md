## 4. Beginner Knowledge Lens

1. **Capex vs. Opex:** Capital expenditures (capex) are investments in long-term assets like data centers and GPUs. Operating expenses (opex) are day-to-day costs like electricity and salaries. When a company says "we're spending $725B on AI," most of that is capex — it shows up on the balance sheet, not just the income statement.

2. **Why the market sells off on "good news":** If a company says "we're investing heavily in AI," that sounds positive. But if the investment is larger than expected and the payoff is uncertain, investors sell because they worry about lower profits in the near term. This is called "selling the news."

3. **Supply chain bottlenecks:** The AI boom isn't just about Nvidia designing good chips. It's about having enough memory (HBM from SK Hynix), enough power (grid capacity), enough cooling (data center infrastructure), and enough networking (optical interconnects from companies like CRDO). The weakest link determines the whole system's speed.

4. **Credit ratings and bond markets:** Moody's warning about AI spending threatening credit quality means that even Apple, Amazon, and Google could face higher borrowing costs if bond investors get nervous. This is a check on how much debt they can take on.

5. **Geopolitics and supply chains:** The US-China tech war means that companies like TSMC, Samsung, and SK Hynix are caught between two superpowers. Export controls, sanctions, and tariffs can disrupt supply chains overnight. South Korea's semiconductor boom is at risk because it depends on both US technology and Chinese demand.

6. **The "show me" market:** When stocks trade at high valuations (high price-to-earnings ratios), the market demands proof that future growth will materialize. If AI spending is high but AI revenue is uncertain, stocks get punished. This is the dynamic playing out right now with mega-cap tech.

## 5. Terms Used Today

1. **Capex (Capital Expenditure):** Money spent to acquire or upgrade physical assets like buildings, machinery, or data centers. *Example: Microsoft spending $50B on AI data centers is capex.*

2. **HBM (High-Bandwidth Memory):** A type of memory that is stacked vertically to save space and provide extremely fast data transfer. Essential for AI GPUs. *Rough benchmark: HBM3e can transfer data at ~1 TB/s. For context, a typical SSD does ~5 GB/s.*

3. **Distillation (AI context):** A technique where a smaller, cheaper AI model is trained to mimic a larger, more capable one. *Example: A distilled version of GPT-4 might be 90% as capable but costs 10% to run.*

4. **Credit Quality:** A measure of a borrower's ability
