# Laksh's Daily Briefing — September 14, 2026

## 1. World & Geopolitics

### CEOs Call for AI Slowdown — and the World Reacts

The biggest story today is not a single event but a coordinated escalation. Over the weekend and continuing today, the CEOs of the three leading frontier AI labs — **Sam Altman (OpenAI), Dario Amodei (Anthropic), and Elon Musk (xAI)** — all publicly called for a slowdown in the development of increasingly powerful AI models. This is unprecedented: the people building the most advanced AI systems are now asking for the brakes to be applied.

Amodei was the most direct, saying the industry "must slow the pace at which we improve the capabilities of AI models," citing concerns that progress could outpace society's ability to deploy the technology safely ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/14/ai-stocks-slowdown-amodei-altman.html)). Altman elaborated on how safety frameworks could work, warning that without guardrails, "we could lose control" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/14/sam-altman-ai-slowdown-anthropic-amodei-musk.html)). The push is getting political: Former President Obama urged Democrats to make AI safeguards a "central agenda" item ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/13/obama-urges-democrats-to-have-a-clear-plan-for-ai-safeguards/)), while President Trump lashed out, saying there is "no need for more AI regulation" and slamming Amodei ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/14/trump-ai-data-centers-anthropic-dario-amodei.html)). Trump's stance aligns with the deregulatory approach his administration has taken on tech.

China immediately seized on the division. State media accused the US tech CEOs of "fear mongering" and argued that all nations should work together for "AI openness and inclusivity" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/14/china-ai-slowdown-us-tech-ceos.html)). This fits a long pattern: Beijing argues that the US wants to slow AI to cement its technological dominance, while China promotes a "sovereign AI" model where every country develops its own independent AI capabilities — a framing that plays well in the Global South. The response highlights a fundamental tension: calls for safety can be weaponized geopolitically as attempts to maintain a technology gap.

## 2. Markets, Money & Deals

### AI Stocks Plunge as Slowdown Calls Spook Markets

The most dramatic market move today: a broad selloff in AI-related stocks triggered by the CEO slowdown calls. Nvidia (NVDA) fell **3.6%**, Broadcom (AVGO) **4.9%**, and Marvell (MRVL) **7.6%**. The selloff was broad but concentrated in companies that benefit directly from frontier model training demand — GPU makers, networking companies, and memory makers. The reaction is logical: if frontier models slow down, the demand for the most advanced GPUs and data center infrastructure might not grow as fast as expected. Citigroup warned that if AI model development slows, "earnings revisions could follow, and those have been a crucial factor in stock gains this year" ([MarketWatch](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/ai-has-carried-the-stock-market-an-industry-pause-could-pull-the-rug-out-warns-this-wall-street-giant-3272c960?mod=mw_rss_topstories)). ### 10-Year Treasury Yield Hits 5%

The yield on the 10-year US Treasury note rose to **5%** for the first time since 2023, a critical psychological level ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/14/10-year-us-treasury-is-closing-in-on-5percent.html)). Reminder: yields move opposite to bond prices; when the yield rises, bond prices fall. The 10-year yield is the benchmark for everything — mortgage rates, corporate borrowing costs, and the discount rate used to value stocks. A 5% yield means the "risk-free" rate (what you can earn on US government debt) is now high enough to compete seriously with stocks for investor money.

The yield surge is driven by expectations that the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates at its meeting this Wednesday. Goldman Sachs expects a **25 basis point (bps)** hike — a quarter of a percentage point — but says the Fed may not signal a "consecutive hiking cycle" ([Moomoo](https://www.google.com/url?sa=t&source=web&rct=j&opi=89978449&url=https://www.moomoo.com/community/detail/202609141080&ved=2ahUKEwjUiPiUmeiJAxVcElkFHWQrBcYQFnoECBcQAQ&usg=AOvVaw3c6Rv4b2XSjHkUvVMQN0QG)). The bond market is effectively "pushing for" a hike because falling bond prices (rising yields) signal that investors expect higher rates to combat inflation, including the oil-driven spike. This is crucial: the Fed targets core PCE (inflation excluding food and energy), so oil alone might not trigger a hike — but the broad inflation picture and strong jobs market give them cover to act ([MarketWatch](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/fed-rate-hikes-wont-bring-down-gas-prices-why-the-bond-market-is-pushing-for-them-anyway-98f2c7e3?mod=mw_rss_topstories)).

## 3. AI & Infrastructure

### The Slowdown Call Hits the Infrastructure Buildout

The key question for investors in AI infrastructure: does a slowdown in model development mean a slowdown in the capital expenditure (capex) buildout? The sources are divided. The selloff in GPU stocks suggests the market thinks yes. But the analyst consensus, as expressed in multiple reports, is "probably not in the near term." Hyperscalers have already committed to massive data center construction; those plans will take years to reverse. However, the "growth rate of growth" — how much additional capex gets approved for 2027 and beyond — could slow if frontier labs are voluntarily reducing their model training runs.

There is a second, more subtle dynamic: if the US slows, China accelerates. China is already using the US safety debate as cover to present itself as the champion of open AI development. The Hong Kong IPO boom is partly driven by Chinese AI companies raising capital ([Nikkei](https://www.google.com/url?sa=t&source=web&rct=j&opi=89978449&url=https://asia.nikkei.com/Business/Markets/Hong-Kong-IPO-boom-and-Chinese-AI-race-fuel-frenzy-of-follow-on-deals&ved=2ahUKEwjUiPiUmeiJAxVcElkFHWQrBcYQFnoECBcQAQ&usg=AOvVaw3c6Rv4b2XSjHkUvVMQN0QG)), meaning the gap between US and Chinese AI hardware demand may not close as fast as US-centric investors expect.

### Nvidia: Margin Pressure Despite Dominance

A Motley Fool analysis notes that while Nvidia continues to dominate the GPU market, it faces "massive capex and margin pressure" ([Motley Fool](https://www.google.com/url?sa=t&source=web&rct=j&opi=89978449&url=https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/09/14/nvidia-massive-capex-and-margin-pressure/&ved=2ahUKEwjUiPiUmeiJAxVcElkFHWQrBcYQFnoECBcQAQ&usg=AOvVaw3c6Rv4b2XSjHkUvVMQN0QG)). As it spends to build out its own supply chain and capacity, its profit margins may compress. This is a natural cycle: high demand leads to investment, which increases supply, which eventually brings down prices. The margin compression story is one reason Nvidia fell more than other AI names today.

### Teravolt: Cannibalizing Older Industries for AI Power

A startup called Teravolt is looking to "cannibalize older industries" to meet AI's enormous power demand ([The Register](https://www.google.com/url?sa=t&source=web&rct=j&opi=89978449&url=https://www.theregister.com/2026/09/14/teravolt_cannibalize_older_industries_ai_power/&ved=2ahUKEwjUiPiUmeiJAxVcElkFHWQrBcYQFnoECBcQAQ&usg=AOvVaw3c6Rv4b2XSjHkUvVMQN0QG)). The specific approach: buying older, less efficient power plants and retooling them to run dedicated data center power. This is the kind of creative engineering that will define the next phase of the buildout — there is not enough new grid capacity, so companies must repurpose old assets.

### Kraken (Crypto Exchange) Eyes Nuclear Power for Data Centers

Crypto exchange Kraken is exploring nuclear power for data centers, anticipating a DeFi (decentralized finance) boom that will require massive compute ([Yellow.com](https://www.google.com/url?sa=t&source=web&rct=j&opi=89978449&url=https://www.yellow.com/crypto/kraken-nuclear-power-data-centers-2026/&ved=2ahUKEwjUiPiUmeiJAxVcElkFHWQrBcYQFnoECBcQAQ&usg=AOvVaw3c6Rv4b2XSjHkUvVMQN0QG)). This is important because it links two trends: the crypto industry's need for energy, and the emerging willingness of energy-intensive industries to pursue dedicated nuclear generation. Small modular reactors (SMRs) are the medium-term hope.

### GM Plans US Battery Development

General Motors announced plans to develop its own US-based battery supply chains for both EVs and energy storage, responding to Trump administration pressure on automakers to reduce dependence on China for battery raw materials ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/12/gm-us-battery-development-ford-china-ties.html)). ### 10-Year Yield at 5%: The Data Center Cost Impact

The 5% yield on the 10-year Treasury matters hugely for the AI buildout because most data center construction is financed with debt. Higher borrowing costs make every new gigawatt of capacity more expensive. If the Fed continues hiking, the "cost of borrowing" for hyperscalers and neoclouds rises, potentially slowing the pace of new construction.

## 5. What Builders Are Actually Using

- **Concrete swap:** Smaug Mini (open-weight, from Abacus.AI, 8B parameters) can stand in for GPT-4o-mini on simple classification and data extraction tasks. The capability gap is real but narrow: Smaug Mini scores ~85% of GPT-4o-mini's accuracy on standard benchmarks but costs **0** (free, self-hosted on a single $300 GPU or Apple Silicon Mac) vs GPT-4o-mini at **$0.15 per million input tokens**. For high-volume, low-stakes tasks, the cost savings are enormous. This is not from today's news — it is carried forward from the Abacus.AI release on Sep 10, which was covered in the source bundle.

- **Self-hosting at scale is getting cheaper and easier.** The RTX PRO 5500 Blackwell (84GB VRAM) was released, making it possible to run large open-weight models locally without cloud costs ([r/LocalLLaMA](https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/1wfxi36/rtx_pro_5500_blackwell_84gb_released/)). This is significant because 84GB is enough to run a 70B-parameter quantized model (4-bit quantization), which would have required a multi-GPU setup or cloud rental before.

- **A solo developer wired ZLUDA to AMD's HIP libraries for Windows, enabling CUDA-dependent AI workloads to run on AMD GPUs** without virtualization or dual-booting ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpu-drivers/solo-developer-wires-zluda-to-amds-hip-getting-multiple-cuda-libraries-running-on-a-radeon-rx-9060-xt-in-windows-cuda-exclusive-workloads-on-amd-hardware-in-windows-is-possible-without-virtualization-or-dual-booting)). This is a small but significant practical bridge over Nvidia's CUDA moat — if it matures, it would let developers use AMD GPUs (which are generally cheaper and more available) to run Nvidia-optimized code.

- **OpenArch is a new GitHub repo collecting PyTorch implementations of modern LLM architectures** — a practical resource for developers building custom models or fine-tuning existing ones ([GitHub](https://github.com/anuj0456/OpenArch); [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49693384)). It has 101 points on HN, suggesting genuine community interest.

## 7. Analysis: Second-Order Effects

**Chain 1: AI CEO slowdown call -> market re-prices AI infrastructure growth -> GPU/optics/memory stocks fall -> higher rates from Fed compound the selloff -> capital allocation for data centers becomes more expensive -> the marginal project gets delayed -> supply of GPUs catches up with demand faster than expected -> Nvidia's margins compress sooner.**

FACTS: Three AI CEOs called for a slowdown today. AI stocks fell 3-11%. The 10-year Treasury yield hit 5% and the Fed is expected to raise rates on Wednesday.

INFERENCE: The market is pricing in two separate but reinforcing forces. The first is a demand-side shock: if frontier labs voluntarily reduce training, demand for the most advanced hardware may grow slower than the 30-40% annual capex growth investors have been pricing. The second is a cost-of-capital shock: higher borrowing costs make new data center construction more expensive, which will push the rate of return (ROI) on new capacity below the threshold some hyperscalers require.

WEAKEST LINK: The assumption that "voluntary slowdown" actually reduces hardware demand. In practice, frontier labs may continue buying GPUs at the same rate but using them for safety research or inference rather than training. The hardware demand is for compute, not specifically for training.

FALSIFYING CONDITION: If Nvidia's data center revenue guidance for Q3 exceeds expectations, the slowdown call will have proven to be noise for hardware demand. Watch for Nvidia's next earnings report (expected in November).

**Chain 2: Oil above $109, Strait of Hormuz blocked -> countries substitute coal for oil/gas -> global coal demand rises to record -> CO2 emissions rise -> carbon offset prices and ESG compliance costs increase -> European energy-intensive industries (steel, chemicals, cement) face higher costs -> this reduces eurozone growth -> the ECB is more cautious on rate cuts -> the euro weakens vs the dollar -> US exports become more expensive -> US multinational earnings (in USD terms) are hurt.**

FACTS: Brent crude above $109. Saudi Arabia's backup pipeline is shut. Iran shot down a US drone. Hormuz talks postponed. IEA forecasts record coal demand.

INFERENCE: The energy system is adapting to the Hormuz disruption by choosing the dirtiest fuel available. This is a classic "energy trilemma" moment — the tradeoff among security, cost, and environment. When security is threatened, cost and environment both suffer. The environmental cost (higher CO2) will have regulatory second-order effects, especially in Europe where carbon pricing (the EU ETS, or Emissions Trading System) is already high. The economic cost (higher energy prices for European industry) will slow growth.

WEAKEST LINK: The assumption that higher coal demand leads to higher EU carbon prices. In a recession, industrial production falls, so carbon demand drops even if carbon intensity per unit rises. The net effect on carbon prices is ambiguous.

FALSIFYING CONDITION: If the Strait of Hormuz reopens and Saudi Arabia restarts its pipeline within a week, this chain evaporates. Watch for diplomatic progress — if Oman-Hormuz talks are rescheduled quickly, the risk premium will collapse.

**Chain 3: Fed rate hike this week -> 10-year yield stays at 5% or goes higher -> mortgage rates follow -> US housing market slows -> homebuilder stocks fall -> consumer confidence drops as housing wealth effect reverses -> retail spending weakens -> earnings for consumer discretionary companies (Nike, Starbucks) are depressed -> the Fed's goal of slowing the economy is achieved -> the Fed stops hiking sooner than the market expects -> bond yields peak and then fall -> stocks rally on "peak rates" narrative.**

FACTS: The Fed is widely expected to raise rates by 25bp on Wednesday. The 10-year yield is at 5%. Mortgage rates have a strong correlation with the 10-year yield.

INFERENCE: This is the standard "tightening cycle" chain that has played out many times before. The lag between a rate hike and its effect on the real economy is 6-18 months. The housing market is the most sensitive to rates because it relies on borrowing. If the Fed raises rates and keeps them high, the housing market and consumer spending will slow. But the market is forward-looking: if investors believe the Fed will stop after this hike (Goldman Sachs expects no "consecutive hiking cycle"), then the 10-year yield may have already peaked. This is why stocks have rallied on some Fed decision days in recent history.

WEAKEST LINK: The assumption that oil-driven inflation does not force the Fed to keep hiking beyond this one. If oil stays above $100, headline inflation will remain high, and the Fed's preferred measure (core PCE) might still nudge up as higher energy costs ripple through the economy (transportation costs affect goods prices with a lag). The Fed could be forced into a series of hikes, not just one.

FALSIFYING CONDITION: If the Fed's statement on Wednesday explicitly leaves the door open for further hikes (hawkish language), the 10-year yield will likely breach 5% and move toward 5.25-5.5%. If instead the statement signals "one and done" (dovish language), yields may fall back to 4.8% or below.

## 8. Building Your Knowledge

- **When AI CEOs call for a slowdown, it is not purely altruistic.** There are commercial incentives: slowing the pace means their existing models stay competitive longer, they have more time to build safety systems that justify their valuations, and it raises barriers to entry for competitors. The geopolitical framing (US vs China) means a slowdown that applies to only US companies could be strategically catastrophic. This is a rare case where corporate self-interest, public safety, and geopolitics all point in the same direction — but for different reasons.

- **The yield on the 10-year Treasury hitting 5% is a psychological event for markets.** There is no fundamental difference between 4.99% and 5.00%, but the market treats round numbers as resistance levels. In 2023, the 10-year briefly touched 5%; stocks sold off, then rallied. In 2024, it stayed below 5%. Now it is back. The level matters because it determines the discount rate used in stock valuation models: a higher discount rate lowers the present value of future earnings, which makes any high-growth stock (read: AI names) look less attractive.

- **The split in AI stock performance today (Nvidia fell, Google rose) teaches a key investing lesson: "picks and shovels" vs "the miners."** Nvidia sells the picks and shovels (GPUs) to the gold rush. If the gold rush slows (fewer frontier models being trained), demand for picks and shovels falls directly. But Google and Microsoft are the miners — they operate the services that sell AI to customers. Even if training slows, their existing models can be deployed at scale for inference, and the cloud infrastructure they have built continues to generate revenue. The miner is less exposed to the pace of new discovery; the pick-and-shovel seller is entirely exposed.

- **A "war-risk premium" in oil is insurance that the market buys and holds.** It is not based on actual supply disruption but on the probability of disruption. Today, the probability is high (drone shot down, pipeline shut, talks collapsed). If the probability drops (ceasefire in Iran conflict), the premium can vanish in hours, taking oil prices down sharply. This is a double-edged sword for anyone trying to trade oil: the price already contains a large uncertainty premium, so the move when that uncertainty resolves is often violent.

- **Data center buildout is a multi-year commitment that cannot be quickly reversed.** Even if AI model training slowed tomorrow, hyperscalers have contracted for power, ordered substations, and poured concrete for buildings that will take 2-3 years to complete. The question is not "will they cancel?" but "will they order the next wave of expansion?" The selloff today is about that marginal decision, not about existing projects.

## 9. Foundations & Terms

### Concept Spotlight: Negative Carry (Markets/Investing)

**What it is in plain words:** Negative carry happens when the cost of holding an asset or position is higher than the income that asset generates. You pay more to keep the position than you get back from it. It is like owning a rental apartment that costs you $2,000 a month in mortgage and fees, but you only collect $1,500 in rent — you are "carrying" a $500 monthly loss.

**Why it exists:** Negative carry arises in finance when traders and investors take positions not for current income but because they expect a future price move. They are willing to lose money in the short term (the "carry cost") to profit in the long term (the "capital gain"). It is the opposite of "positive carry," where the asset generates more income than its cost.

**Concrete example:** Suppose you buy a 10-year Treasury bond with a yield of 5%, but you finance that purchase by borrowing at 6% in the short-term repo market. Your "net carry" is -1% per year: you earn 5% on the bond but pay 6% on the loan. You lose 1% annually just to hold the position. Why would you do this? Because you expect bond yields to fall (prices to rise), and the capital gain when you sell the bond will more than offset the -1% carry loss. This is called a "steepener" or "duration" trade.

**How it shows up in the news:** In today's brief, the Fed is about to raise rates. Investors who own long-duration AI stocks (which are essentially assets that promise earnings far in the future) are experiencing negative carry: they could sell those stocks and put the money in a 5% Treasury, earning a safe 5% instead of waiting for uncertain future AI profits. The negative carry of holding AI stocks relative to Treasuries is one reason the selloff happened.

**Rough numbers to remember:** If the 10-year Treasury yields 5%, and the "equity risk premium" (the extra return investors demand for owning stocks instead of bonds) is historically about 3-4%, then stocks need to offer an expected return of 8-9% to be attractive. If investors believe AI stocks can deliver that, the negative carry of holding them (vs bonds) is acceptable. If they lose faith in that expected return, they sell and take the 5% safe yield.

**Caution/common misconception:** Negative carry does not mean a trade is bad; it means the trade has specific timing requirements. You must be right about the direction of the price move, and the move must happen before your cumulative carry costs eat your capital. Many hedge fund blow-ups have come from trades that were "right" on direction but took too long to play out, and the carry costs wiped out the fund first.

### Concept Spotlight: Buffer State (World Affairs)

**What it is in plain words:** A buffer state is a country located between two larger, more powerful countries (or blocs). Its function is to separate the rivals, reducing the chance of direct conflict between them. Think of it as a neutral cushion between two heavyweights.

**Why it exists:** Buffer states exist because of the fundamental logic of great-power competition: neighbors fight more than distant countries. If two rival great powers share a direct border, every military patrol, every troop movement, every minor incident can escalate into full war. A buffer state provides a physical and political separation. The great powers avoid touching each other directly, which reduces friction.

**Concrete example:** Ukraine is a classic buffer state between Russia and NATO. For centuries, the region that is now Ukraine was contested between the Russian Empire and European powers. After the Cold War, Ukraine's independence created a buffer between Russia and NATO's eastern expansion. When Ukraine sought to join NATO, Russia lost its buffer, and one cause of the 2022 invasion was Russia's demand that Ukraine remain neutral as a permanent buffer. In today's brief, Kushner said "territory remains the main obstacle" to a peace deal — because the buffer (the land between Russia and NATO) is exactly what is being fought over.

**How it shows up in the news:** The concept of buffer states is present in every major geopolitical conflict: Taiwan as a buffer between China and the US; the South China Sea islands as potential buffers; Nepal and Mongolia as buffers between China and India/Russia respectively. In today's sources, Nepal's decision not to join BRICS (in favor of a long-term defense partnership with the US) can be read as a small buffer state carefully managing its position between India and China ([Himalayan Times](https://www.google.com/url?sa=t&source=web&rct=j&opi=89978449&url=https://thehimalayantimes.com/business/nepal-strategic-non-participation-in-brics&ved=2ahUKEwjUiPiUmeiJAxVcElkFHWQrBcYQFnoECBcQAQ&usg=AOvVaw3c6Rv4b2XSjHkUvVMQN0QG)).

**Rough numbers/benchmarks worth remembering:** The most studied buffer states in modern history: Ukraine (between Russia and NATO); Afghanistan (historically between Russia/Britain, later Russia/US); Laos and Cambodia (between Vietnam, China, and Thailand); Finland (between Russia and Sweden/NATO — Finland's choice to join NATO in 2023 ended its buffer status directly on Russia's border). When a buffer state disappears, the great powers suddenly share a border, which historically has increased the risk of direct conflict.

**Caution/common misconception:** Buffer states are not always weak or passive. Some, like Switzerland, have maintained neutrality for centuries by being defensible and economically useful to both sides. The concept of a "neutral" buffer state is different from a "client" buffer state — a true buffer is independent, while a client is effectively controlled by one great power, making it useless as a separator.
