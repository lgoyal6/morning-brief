### 2.2 China's DUV Lithography: A Threat to Memory and ASML's Dominance
China's domestic **DUV lithography machines** entering service is a seismic shift in the semiconductor supply chain. DUV lithography is used to make **DRAM and NAND flash memory** (SK Hynix, Samsung, Micron, Sandisk). Until now, the only supplier of advanced DUV tools was ASML (Netherlands), restricted by US export controls. If China can produce its own DUV machines, it can:
- Build memory chips **without relying on Western equipment**.
- Potentially flood the market with lower-cost memory, crushing margins for Korean and US makers.
- Reduce ASML's future revenue growth.

This explains why Samsung and SK Hynix shares plunged, and why MU (-8.7%) and SNDK (-14%) sold off. However, Chinese DUV tools are likely **less advanced** than ASML's — the immediate risk is more about sentiment than actual disruption. The second-order effect: if memory prices collapse, HBM (high-bandwidth memory) for AI could also get cheaper, which benefits Nvidia but hurts memory makers.
