## 3. AI & Infrastructure

**The Akamai-Anthropic deal is the AI infrastructure story of the day.** Let's unpack the $11.6 billion deal. Anthropic, one of the leading AI labs (makers of the Claude models), is paying Akamai to use its edge network to serve AI inference. Akamai's CEO suggests it's a "No Nvidia needed" approach, meaning they can run inference on less-specialized hardware distributed around the world ([Investor's Business Daily](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMisAFBVV95cUxOa1NUaWJ2RG9tbHFXVUlhcDhBVW1xVWJkVnItUU9NekVFUkhJUTM4cHRGUHRBZlJLTHRfbDA3SkJEMHFydFJjT25iX0Jhc28tdWJxQkpFY0dBbjVtREFHb1dIWGI1a0NuR3pXVjhXbUR2Njlra0liRFZvem1sWGJtRmFYbE00T1NBc1VKYlFpbEFFcExOWWkzckdYOXJvYlBkX3hCeDViWHY5clgyMFo1bw?oc=5)). Why this matters for the buildout: it shows a second path to AI compute beyond buying thousands of Nvidia GPUs for centralized data centers. It also validates that AI workloads will run at the "edge" (close to users) for latency reasons, which is good for Akamai, Cloudflare (NET), and other edge networking players. Anthropic gets capacity without building its own data centers, a sign that even top AI labs remain compute-hungry. The details of the deal matter, notably whether it's a committed contract or a framework agreement.

**Oracle sends a force majeure notice on its New Mexico "Stargate" data center.** Oracle (ORCL) has sent a force majeure (a legal clause that frees parties from obligations when extraordinary events occur) notice on its data center at the Stargate project, which would allow it to delay payments if the facility misses its 2028 target to come online ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/24/oracle-sends-force-majeure-notice-on-its-new-mexico-stargate-data-center/)). This is a big deal for two reasons. First, it signals that even the biggest AI infrastructure projects are facing delays, whether from power constraints, construction issues, or supply chain problems. Second, "force majeure" is a term worth knowing: it's the legal escape hatch for when "acts of God" or similar extraordinary events prevent a contract from being fulfilled. For Laksh: delays in AI data center buildout are a risk to the entire AI trade, because the market has priced in massive capacity growth. Watch for more force majeure notices.

**The $10 billion per gigawatt capex fight: Arm AGI CPU vs AMD EPYC.** A detailed piece on shattered.io examines the claim that Arm-based "AGI CPUs" could save up to $10 billion per gigawatt of AI data center capacity compared to standard x86 servers (like AMD's EPYC) ([shattered.io](https://shattered.io/arm-agi-cpu-vs-amd-epyc-data-center-race-2026)). Arm (ARM) is positioning its CPU designs as more power-efficient than AMD's (AMD) and Intel's (INTC). This is a real competitive battle, and the winner will capture huge value. For Laksh: power efficiency is becoming the key battleground in the AI buildout, alongside raw compute.

**Hyperscaler capex is projected to reach nearly $800 billion in 2026 and $1.3 trillion in 2027.** NVIDIA's CEO said a 1-gigawatt AI factory rents for $50 billion a year, about what it costs to build it ([247wallst](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/24/nvidias-ceo-says-a-1-gigawatt-ai-factory-rents-for-50-billion-a-year-as-much-as-it-costs-to-build)). This is astonishing math: the revenue from renting AI compute is so high that a data center can pay for itself in about a year. ## 5. What Builders Are Actually Using

**The concrete swap today: Xiaomi MiMo-V2.6-4B can stand in for a mid-tier closed model like OpenAI's GPT-4o-mini for many tasks, at a fraction of the cost.** MiMo-V2.6 is fully open-weights (Apache-style license), so you can download it and run it yourself for free on a single high-end GPU (like an RTX 4090) or even a lower-end one for chat use cases. By contrast, GPT-4o-mini via API costs roughly $0.15 per million input tokens and $0.60 per million output tokens (approximate pricing). The capability gap: MiMo-V2.6-4B will be worse at complex reasoning, coding, and long-context tasks than GPT-4o-mini, likely a meaningful notch below. But for straightforward tasks, retrieval-augmented generation, and multilingual chat, it is a very credible option, and it runs with zero data leaving your machine. Note that this is a synthesis from today's coverage of the model's release ([The New Stack](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiYkFVX3lxTFBqbVhHcXlRYmdlaHZmLU9qOEZmcXp4LW50dlMzMVBZb2ExZVZzd0xmX3J0bGhacDRmMGhyd1hHQlgwUkcxX2JnTVZxRGFla2ZsaGpyME5nVXpXb1pxNVBpRENn?oc=5)) and the general "best model for every budget" dynamic, not a specific price quote from today's news.

**Model routing is the cheapest performance hack right now.** Instead of sending every query to a frontier model, a router (a piece of software that decides which model to use per query) can send simple requests to a small, cheap model and only escalate hard ones to the big model. This is covered in an accessible 13-step guide ([tech-insider](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMieEFVX3lxTE95Nm1neUpWWnowbTBxNUQ2NFpEOF85NGRVTnctVmdlZ2FDUFVBOWIwWGZDci1KU1M0NHZ1RnIzSEVRTlkyaU9CdjRuVWh4alpvRmxTTENZaXNOTGtPR2MxcWdXZ2F4OHJrWHhHMnBXdnY2QXViVXJsMg?oc=5)). In practice, for a typical RAG app (retrieval-augmented generation, where the model answers based on a document set), routing can cut API costs by 50-80% because maybe 70% of queries are simple. The analogy Laksh should hold: it's like using a scooter for errands and a truck for moving furniture; you don't take the truck to the grocery store.

**Running models locally with LM Studio is now trivial.** LM Studio is a desktop app that lets you download and run open-weights models like Gemma 4 on your own machine without any code. A guide shows how to use it for private business documents ([pasqualepillitteri.it](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMikwFBVV95cUxNdVZHTzhuSkQ5TjlVZ0ZhY2xka1U1djA4a1FUdEE2eUNReUtaQjhBQi1lNjRpZUEtWWw5S2pVTHN3NUE5enhEbzJYZWpINTUxMEFnZGJUU1NBRUxoM2E3cHppYncydnAyYXVRUnhITW5qZ2FwN0Jvb1RzeHk1VUMzRkRUdi03alJHM09QVjZTV21CVTg?oc=5)). This is the "data sovereignty" layer of AI, and it's where the "zero-retention" promise becomes real.

**A practical tool for choosing models: bestmodelforyourbudget.terrydjony.com.** This is a live, community-updated site that ranks the best models for different budget brackets, updated daily ([Hacker News](https://bestmodelforyourbudget.terrydjony.com/)). This is a hugely practical resource because it answers the exact question a builder asks: "Given I want to spend less than $X per million tokens, which model should I use?" It's the kind of tool that institutional press ignores but every engineer has bookmarked.

## 7. Analysis: Second-Order Effects

**Chain 1: Treasury yields surge -> mortgage rates near 8% -> housing market freezes -> consumer confidence drops -> slower economic growth -> earnings expectations get cut -> stock multiples compress further.**

FACTS: The 10-year Treasury yield is at multi-decade highs (sourced above). The 30-year fixed mortgage is surging with 8% "not an impossibility" ([MarketWatch](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/8-mortgage-rates-are-not-an-impossibility-as-the-30-year-fixed-rate-surges-aa202464?mod=mw_rss_topstories)). Back-to-back weak Treasury auctions show lack of demand ([MarketWatch](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/why-investors-arent-buying-yet-another-attempt-by-the-treasury-to-calm-the-rattled-bond-market-b168cac3?mod=mw_rss_topstories)).

INFERENCE: This is the classic transmission mechanism. When the cost of a 30-year mortgage rises from, say, 6.5% to 8%, the monthly payment on a median-priced home rises by hundreds of dollars, which prices out many buyers. Home sales will slow, which hits construction jobs, furniture/appliance spending, and real estate commissions. The wealth effect (people feeling poorer when their home's value stagnates) dampens consumer spending. Weaker consumer spending then hits corporate earnings, and because stock prices are the present value of future earnings discounted at the risk-free rate (the Treasury yield), both lower earnings and a higher discount rate push stocks down. The weakest link: whether the Fed lets this play out or intervenes. If Fed Chairman Warsh decides that a housing crash is acceptable collateral damage in the fight against inflation, the chain holds; if he blinks and cuts rates, the chain breaks.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY IT: A sharp drop in mortgage rates (say, the 30-year fixed back under 6.5%) or an unexpected Fed rate cut within the next month. Also, if housing starts (new home construction) hold steady, that would suggest the housing market is absorbing higher rates better than feared.

Precedent: In the early 1980s, the Fed under Paul Volcker pushed rates to 20% to break inflation, causing a severe recession and a housing bust. It worked, but it took years. The 2026 situation is less extreme, but the pattern is the same.

**Chain 2: Iran offers to reopen Strait of Hormuz -> war-risk premium in oil deflates -> diesel/gasoline prices fall -> headline inflation (CPI) eases -> the Fed has less reason to hike rates -> bond yields ease -> growth stocks rally.**

FACTS: Iranian President offers to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within a week and restart nuclear talks, even as Houthis attack Saudi targets ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/25/us-iran-trump-hormuz-.html)). Oil options market is already pricing in lower prices (contrarian signal) ([MarketWatch](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/the-options-market-is-sending-a-contrarian-signal-about-oil-prices-a8812210?mod=mw_rss_topstories)).

INFERENCE: The Strait of Hormuz is the chokepoint for about 20 million barrels per day of oil, roughly a fifth of global consumption. When it's threatened, the "war-risk premium" (the extra price of oil reflecting the chance of supply disruption) can be many dollars per barrel. If Iran's offer is credible and the strait actually reopens, that premium deflates fast, as it did after the 2019 Abqaiq attack (a strike that took out half of Saudi Arabia's production; Brent moved ~15% in a day, then retraced within weeks). Cheaper oil flows through to gasoline and diesel prices at the pump, which is what headline inflation (CPI, the consumer price index) measures. If inflation cools, the Fed has cover to be less hawkish, which would relieve upward pressure on Treasury yields and hurt the US dollar trade. This is the key counter-narrative to the bond rout. The weakest link: Iran's offer is probably a negotiating tactic, not a sincere commitment. The Houthis are still attacking, and they are a separate actor. Even if Iran opens the strait, the Houthi threat to the Red Sea and Bab el-Mandeb remains.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY IT: A credible announcement from the US and Iran of scheduled talks, a halt in Houthi attacks, and a sustained drop in Brent (say, below $70). Conversely, if the Houthis sink a tanker, this chain inverts.

Precedent: The 2019 Abqaiq attack on Saudi oil facilities moved Brent from about $60 to $71 in a day, then it retraced to pre-attack levels within three weeks as it became clear supply wasn't disrupted. This shows that geopolitical oil spikes are often transitory unless they cause real, sustained supply loss.

**Chain 3: Akamai's $11.6 billion Anthropic deal -> AI inference moves to the edge -> Nvidia's massive data-center growth assumption gets questioned -> the AI supply chain increasingly bifurcates into training (Nvidia-heavy) and inference (distributed, heterogeneous) -> infrastructure winners change.**

FACTS: Akamai surges 15% on the deal; the headline is "No Nvidia Needed" ([Investor's Business Daily](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMisAFBVV95cUxOa1NUaWJ2RG9tbHFXVUlhcDhBVW1xVWJkVnItUU9NekVFUkhJUTM4cHRGUHRBZlJLTHRfbDA3SkJEMHFydFJjT25iX0Jhc28tdWJxQkpFY0dBbjVtREFHb1dIWGI1a0NuR3pXVjhXbUR2Njlra0liRFZvem1sWGJtRmFYbE00T1NBc1VKYlFpbEFFcExOWWkzckdYOXJvYlBkX3hCeDViWHY5clgyMFo1bw?oc=5)). NVIDIA's CEO talks about 1-GW AI factories renting for $50B/year ([247wallst](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/24/nvidias-ceo-says-a-1-gigawatt-ai-factory-rents-for-50-billion-a-year-as-much-as-it-costs-to-build)).

But as more applications go live, the majority of the compute will be for inference (serving requests to users), and inference will increasingly run on more distributed, lower-cost hardware, closer to the user to reduce latency. Akamai's deal is evidence that a company can build a profitable AI inference business without Nvidia's flagship data center GPUs, using its existing edge network. If this model works for Anthropic, a major AI lab, it will be replicated. Over time, this bifurcation could mean Nvidia's growth rate moderates while networking, edge compute, and specialized inference chips (both ASICs, or application-specific integrated circuits, and CPUs) gain share. For complex reasoning tasks, the largest models (which need the biggest GPUs) will remain necessary, and the edge will only capture the simpler tasks. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY IT: If Akamai's management says the deal is small relative to its revenue, or if Anthropic publicly announces it's still buying thousands of Nvidia GPUs for inference, the chain weakens. Also, a big Nvidia earnings beat with raised guidance would suggest the centralization path remains dominant.

Precedent: Think of the shift from mainframe computing (centralized) to client-server (distributed) in the 1980s and 1990s, and then to cloud (re-centralized) in the 2000s. Computing topology moves in waves; the AI buildout is in the middle of a wave, and the direction of the next swell is still contested.

## 8. Building Your Knowledge

- **Treasury yields are the "discount rate" for the whole economy.** When you value any investment, you're asking "what is this future cash flow worth today?" Higher yields mean the present value of future profits is lower, which is why high-growth tech stocks (which promise profits far in the future) are hit hardest when yields rise. A company that makes all its profits next year is less sensitive; a company that profits in 2035 is very sensitive.

- **A summit without a deal is not a failure, it's a holding pattern.** The Trump-Xi summit produced no breakthrough on Taiwan or trade, but it also produced no rupture. In geopolitics, "no bad news" is often the best possible outcome for markets. Stability is a tradable asset.

- **The difference between training and inference is the key to the AI supply chain.** Training is the multi-month process of building a model; inference is the per-query act of using it. Training wants giant, fast GPUs. Inference can tolerate smaller, more distributed hardware, which is why Akamai's edge network deal makes sense. The ratio of training to inference spend will determine which companies win.

- **Open-weights models are a price-discovery mechanism for AI.** When Xiaomi releases a 4B model that beats bigger closed models, it sets a price floor for AI capabilities. This is exactly how hardware commoditization works, and it will put pressure on closed-model pricing and on the perceived need for ever-larger training runs.

- **"Force majeure" is a financial canary.** When companies start invoking it on signature projects (like Oracle's Stargate data center), it's a signal that the project is in trouble. Investors should track these notices because they reveal execution risk in the AI buildout before earnings reports do.

## 9. Foundations & Terms

### Concept Spotlight: The Yield Curve and What It Tells You (Markets/Investing)

In plain words: the yield curve is a line that shows the interest rate (the "yield") the US government pays for borrowing money for different lengths of time, from one month to 30 years.

Why it exists: normally, lenders (including investors who buy government bonds) demand a higher interest rate for lending for longer, because there's more risk that inflation will erode the value of future payments over a longer period. So the curve usually slopes upward: the 2-year yields less than the 10-year, and the 10-year yields less than the 30-year.

A concrete worked example: Imagine the 2-year Treasury yields 4% and the 10-year yields 5%. The spread (the gap) is +100 basis points. Now imagine the 2-year yields 5% and the 10-year yields 4%. The curve has "inverted", meaning short-term borrowing costs more than long-term. Historically, an inverted curve (the 10-year yielding less than the 2-year) has preceded every US recession since the 1970s, usually by 12-18 months.

How it shows up in the news: When you read "Treasury yields soar" like today ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/23/what-happens-to-the-economy-when-treasury-yields-soar.html)), the key is not just the level but the shape. Right now, yields are rising across maturities, which signals the market is worried about inflation and a government debt supply glut. If the market worried about recession, we'd see the 10-year fall relative to the 2-year.

Rough benchmarks worth remembering: A 10-year yield around 4% is "normal" for recent times; 5% is high and restrictive; 3% would signal a severe recession. An inverted curve for over 6 months is a recession warning. A yield difference of more than 100 basis points between the 2-year and the 10-year in either direction is significant.

Caution: the yield curve is a leading indicator, not a timer. It can stay inverted for over a year (it did from mid-2022 to mid-2024) before a recession hits. And with the Fed now hiking rates aggressively under Warsh, the signal is more about the Fed's own policy settings than the market's independent read.

### Concept Spotlight: Strategic Stability (World Affairs)

In plain words: strategic stability is a situation between rival great powers where neither side sees an advantage in starting a major conflict, because doing so would be catastrophic for both. The concept grew out of the Cold War, when the US and the Soviet Union had enough nuclear weapons to destroy each other, which paradoxically kept the peace.

Why it exists: the problem it solves is how to manage a rivalry without fighting. When two powers can destroy each other (mutual assured destruction), they are "stably" at peace, because the cost of conflict outweighs any possible benefit. The term now applies more broadly, to a stable equilibrium where neither the US and China nor any other pair of rivals takes actions that could spiral into war.

A concrete worked example: In the Cold War, the US and USSR signed arms control treaties (like SALT) that capped the number of nuclear missiles each could have. The treaties didn't end the rivalry, but they made the rivalry predictable and limited the risk of accidental or miscalculated escalation.

How it shows up in today's news: The Trump-Xi summit is an exercise in maintaining strategic stability ([Defense Priorities](https://www.defensepriorities.org/press-releases/trump-xi-summit-should-build-on-constructive-strategic-stability-framework)). The two countries disagree on Taiwan, trade, and AI, but they both want to avoid a direct military clash. The "constructive strategic stability" framework agreed in Beijing in May is precisely this: a set of rules (like military hotlines and channeling disputes through summit talks) that prevents a crisis from becoming a war. It's a fancy term for "we agree to keep talking even though we deeply distrust each other".

Rough benchmarks worth remembering: Strategic stability is less about numbers and more about communication channels and predictability. Deterrence (making an attack too costly) is the foundation; arms control or crisis management mechanisms are the maintenance. When leaders stop meeting and stop communicating, stability erodes.

Caution: strategic stability is not peace. It's a managed rivalry. The US and China remain fundamentally adversarial on Taiwan and the tech war; stability just means the rivalry is contained. And stability can break down quickly if a miscalculation (say, over Taiwan or a naval incident) escalates beyond political control.

### Other Terms from Today's News

- **Force majeure:** A legal clause in a contract that frees both parties from liability or obligation when an extraordinary event (beyond their control) happens, such as a natural disaster or war. - **Content-delivery network (CDN):** The original business of Akamai. A system of distributed servers that delivers web content (videos, images, web pages) to users from the server closest to them, speeding up load times. The AI inference play is an evolution: using the same network to run AI queries close to users.

- **Force majeure notice:** the specific written notice a party sends to invoke the force majeure clause. It's a legal signal that a project is in trouble.

- **Parameters (in a model):** The internal numbers of an AI model, set during training, that determine its behavior. Larger parameter counts (like 7 billion or 100 billion) generally mean more capacity to learn, but not always better results, as today's small models show.
