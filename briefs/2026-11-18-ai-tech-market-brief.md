## 5. Foundations & Terms

### Concept Spotlight: Trilateral Summit

**What it is:** A meeting between the leaders of three countries. In geopolitics, a trilateral summit is a rare and high-stakes event, especially between great powers.

**Why it exists:** To manage complex relationships that involve multiple overlapping conflicts. A bilateral meeting (US-China) might not resolve issues involving a third party (Russia). A trilateral allows for direct communication and potential trade-offs.

**Concrete example:** The Trump-Xi-Putin meeting in Shenzhen. The US wants Russia to agree to a ceasefire in Ukraine. China wants the US to ease tariffs. Russia wants China's economic support. The summit allows them to negotiate these linkages directly.

**How it shows up in the news:** As a "historic" or "unprecedented" gathering. Analysts focus on the body language, the length of the meeting, and whether a joint statement is issued.

**A figure worth remembering:** There has been no formal US-China-Russia trilateral summit since the end of the Cold War. The Yalta Conference in 1945 was the last time the three great powers sat down to reshape the world order.

**Common misconception:** That a trilateral summit means the three countries are allies or friends. In reality, it is often a sign of deep strategic competition that needs active management to prevent conflict.

- **Interim CEO:** A temporary chief executive appointed by the board of directors after a sudden departure (firing, resignation, death) while a permanent replacement is sought. The interim CEO has the full powers of the role but usually avoids making major strategic pivots. *Concrete example:* Colette Kress, Nvidia's CFO, was named interim CEO after Jensen Huang was ousted. Her job is to stabilize the company and the stock.
- **Cloud Loophole:** A gap in US export controls that allowed Chinese AI companies to access advanced American chips (like Nvidia H100s) by renting computing power from cloud data centers located in third countries (e.g., Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia). The US is now closing this loophole by requiring those data centers to get a license to buy advanced chips.
- **Go-private merger (Take-private):** A transaction where a publicly traded company is bought by a private equity firm or a group of investors, and its shares are delisted from the stock exchange. Companies do this to escape the short-term pressure of quarterly earnings reports and focus on long-term restructuring. *Concrete example:* Utz Brands, the snack food company, was taken private in a $2.9 billion deal.
- **Panic buying:** A surge in consumer purchasing of essential goods (food, water, batteries, medicine) driven by fear of a future shortage or disaster. It is a leading indicator of consumer anxiety. *Concrete example:* Costco reported a spike in "emergency prep" sales following a series of major tech outages (Microsoft, FAA, NSA) that disrupted daily life.
