## 5. Foundations & Terms

### Concept Spotlight: IPO (Initial Public Offering) and the Anchor Investor

**What it is in plain words:** An IPO (Initial Public Offering) is when a private company sells shares to the general public for the first time and lists them on a stock exchange. It is the moment a company "goes public".

**Why it exists:** Companies do IPOs to raise money (capital) for expansion, and to give early investors (venture capitalists, founders, employees) a way to sell their shares and realize a return on their investment.

**A concrete worked example with real numbers:** Anthropic is expected to price its IPO at $70-$78 per share. Let's say it prices at $74 and sells 35 million shares. It raises $2.59 billion. The company's "market cap" (total value of a company's outstanding shares) at the IPO price is the number of shares outstanding times $74.

**Anchor Investors:** Tech giants (Nvidia, Google, Microsoft, etc.) are investing $26 billion *at the IPO price*. This is an "anchor investment" -- a large, strategic purchase by a big institution at the IPO price. It signals confidence to the market, helps ensure the IPO is fully subscribed (all shares are sold), and locks them in as long-term strategic partners. It is a massive vote of confidence.

**Lock-Up Period:** After an IPO, insiders (founders, early investors, employees) are usually prohibited from selling their shares for a period of time (typically 90 to 180 days). This is the "lock-up period". It prevents a flood of shares from hitting the market immediately after the IPO, which would crash the price. When the lock-up expires, the stock often dips because insiders can finally sell.

**A figure worth remembering:** The $26B anchor investment in Anthropic is roughly 3x the total amount raised in the entire US IPO market in a typical slow month. It shows how strategically important AI is to these giants.

**A common misconception:** The IPO price is not the "true value" of the company. It is a price negotiated between the company, its bankers, and anchor investors. The stock can trade wildly above or below that price on its first day (the "pop" or the "flop").

This is distinct from a bilateral (two countries) or multilateral (many countries) summit. It is rare for the leaders of the world's three biggest nuclear powers to meet like this.
