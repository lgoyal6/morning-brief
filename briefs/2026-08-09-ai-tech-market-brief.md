### **Iran keeps Strait of Hormuz closed, demands compensation and sanctions relief**  
Iran’s Revolutionary Guards declared the Strait of Hormuz will remain closed until the U.S. meets a list of demands: lifting sanctions, ending its naval blockade of Iranian ports, and paying compensation for war damages ([France 24](https://www.france24.com/en/middle-east/20260809-iran-vows-to-keep-strait-of-hormuz-closed-until-us-meets-demands)). - **Background**: The Strait of Hormuz is a **chokepoint** — a narrow passage through which ~20% of the world’s oil flows. Closing it is a massive escalation.  
- **Why it matters**: Oil prices spiked again overnight. Brent crude is now up ~12% this week. Even if the closure is temporary, it shows Iran can disrupt global energy flows at will.  
- **Who wins/loses**: Iran gains leverage; oil importers (especially in Asia) lose. The U.S. and its allies lose credibility if they can’t reopen the strait without concessions.  
- **Uncertain**: Will the U.S. use force? So far, President Trump is taking a “low-key” approach, but that could change if the closure persists.

## 7. Analysis: Second-Order Effects

### **Chain 1: Iran closes Strait of Hormuz → oil prices rise → inflation rises → Fed holds rates → higher borrowing costs → AI data center buildout slows**  
- **Facts**: Iran has closed Hormuz; Brent crude up 12% this week.  
- **Inference**: Higher oil → higher headline inflation → Fed less likely to cut rates.  
- **Weakest link**: The Fed targets **core PCE inflation**, which excludes energy. So oil spikes don’t always move the needle.  
- **What would falsify it**: If core PCE stays below 3%, the Fed could still cut.  
- **Precedent**: In 2019, the Abqaiq attack spiked oil 15%, but core PCE didn’t budge, and the Fed cut rates.

### **Chain 2: Moonshot AI’s 2.8T model → open-weight pressure → U.S. labs lower prices → cloud AI margins compress → hyperscalers (AWS, Azure) gain pricing power**  
- **Facts**: Moonshot’s model is open and high-performing.  
- **Inference**: U.S. labs may cut prices to compete, hurting their margins.  
- **Weakest link**: Open models still lack the polish and tooling of closed ones.  
- **What would falsify it**: If U.S. labs maintain premium pricing despite open competition.

### **Chain 3: Apple tests CXMT memory → supply chain diversification → Micron (MU) loses leverage → Chinese memory firms scale faster**  
- **Facts**: Apple is testing Chinese memory.  
- **Inference**: If adopted, it validates Chinese tech and weakens U.S. export control impact.  
- **Weakest link**: Apple may only use CXMT as a backup, not primary.  
- **What would falsify it**: If Apple publicly commits to U.S. suppliers only.

## 8. Building Your Knowledge

- **Market cap** is the true measure of a company’s size, not share price. A $10 stock with 1 billion shares is bigger than a $100 stock with 5 million shares.
- **OPEC+** sets oil prices by coordinating production cuts or increases among member countries.
- **Hybrid warfare** includes cyberattacks, sabotage, and disinformation — not just traditional military action.
- **Core PCE** is the Fed’s preferred inflation gauge because it strips out volatile food and energy prices.

## 9. Foundations & Terms

### **Concept Spotlight: Market Capitalisation**  
- **What it is**: Share price × number of shares outstanding.  
- **Why it exists**: To measure a company’s total value, not just the price of one share.  
- **Example**: Apple has a $3 trillion market cap. That means all its shares combined are worth $3 trillion.  
- **In the news**: TSMC’s market cap is ~$2.1 trillion (4.9B shares × $420).  
- **Benchmarks**: Mega-cap = >$200B; large-cap = $10B–$200B.  
- **Caution**: Market cap can be inflated by hype, not fundamentals.

### **Concept Spotlight: OPEC and OPEC+**  
- **What it is**: A cartel of oil-producing countries that agree on production levels to influence prices.  
- **Why it exists**: To avoid price wars and stabilize revenue.  
- **Example**: In 2020, OPEC+ cut production by 9.7 million barrels/day to stop prices from collapsing.  
- **In the news**: Iran’s Hormuz threat is a way to pressure OPEC+ members like Saudi Arabia.  
- **Benchmarks**: OPEC produces ~40% of the world’s oil.  
- **Caution**: Not all members follow the quotas — cheating can undermine the group.

- **Chokepoint**: A narrow geographic passage critical for global trade, like the Strait of Hormuz.  
- **Capex**: Capital expenditure — money spent on long-term assets like factories or data centers.  
- **Open-weights model**: An AI model whose internal parameters (weights) are publicly available.  
- **HBM**: High-Bandwidth Memory — a type of fast memory used in AI chips.  
- **Hyperscaler**: A giant cloud provider like AWS, Azure, or Google Cloud.  
- **Sandbox (AI security)**: A restricted environment where AI models are tested to prevent harmful behavior.
