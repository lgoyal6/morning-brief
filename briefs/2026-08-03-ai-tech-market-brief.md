**Key papers from today's arXiv:**
- **ThinkReset** (arXiv 2607.28642): A method for long-horizon reasoning that avoids context overflow by creating reusable intermediate interfaces. Practical for AI agents that need to plan over many steps.
- **LARA** (arXiv 2607.28669): Lightweight Additive Residual Adaptation — a more efficient alternative to LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation). Instead of modifying weights, it adds corrections to the hidden state, potentially reducing memory and compute for fine-tuning.

## 7. Analysis: Second-Order Effects

### Chain 1: Iran-US talks stall cycle → oil price volatility → inflation → Fed policy → data center buildout costs

**FACTS:** Trump says he called off strikes on Iran and claims talks are ongoing, but Iran denies them ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/03/trump-iran-us-negotiations-peace-proposals-.html), [PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/trump-frequently-announces-hes-halting-strikes-in-iran-but-they-keep-happening)). The manufacturing survey shows inflation worries "worse than pandemic era" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/03/manufacturing-survey-shows-inflation-worries-adding-to-pressure-on-fed.html)).

**CHAIN:** Iran uncertainty → oil markets price in a risk premium → Brent crude stays elevated → headline inflation (energy costs) remains sticky → Fed holds rates higher for longer → higher borrowing costs for capital-intensive projects → data center construction (which requires massive debt) becomes more expensive → some projects are delayed or cancelled → slower AI infrastructure buildout → Nvidia, AMD, and power stocks face headwinds.

## 9. Foundations & Terms

### Concept Spotlight: Share (Stock)

**What it is:** A share (also called a stock) is a unit of ownership in a company. When you buy one share of Amazon, you own a tiny fraction of Amazon — including a claim on its assets and earnings.

**What owning a share actually entitles you to:**
- **A proportional claim on profits:** If Amazon makes a profit, it can either reinvest it (which should increase the stock price) or distribute it to shareholders as a **dividend**. Not all companies pay dividends — Amazon never has; it reinvests everything.
- **Voting rights:** Most common shares give you one vote per share on major corporate decisions, like electing the board of directors. In practice, small shareholders rarely vote, but the right exists.
- **A claim on assets if the company liquidates:** If Amazon goes bankrupt, shareholders are last in line to get paid (after bondholders and other creditors). In most bankruptcies, shareholders get nothing.
- **The right to sell:** You can sell your share to anyone else at whatever price the market offers. That's where the "stock price" comes from — it's the last price at which a trade happened.

**Why it exists:** Companies need money to grow — to build factories, hire engineers, develop products. They can borrow (debt) or sell ownership (equity). Selling shares is a way to raise capital without taking on debt that must be repaid. Investors buy shares because they expect the company to grow and the share price to rise, or because they want the dividend income.

**Concrete worked example:**
- You buy 100 shares of Amazon at $284.12 each (today's price). Cost: $28,412.
- If Amazon's stock rises to $300, your shares are worth $30,000 → a gain of $1,588.
- If Amazon doesn't pay dividends, your only return is from price appreciation.
- If Amazon's stock falls to $250, you've lost $3,412.

**How it shows up in the news:** "Amazon tops $3 trillion market cap" — that means the total value of all Amazon shares (market cap = share price × number of shares) is $3 trillion. The news is about the price, not about Amazon's cash.

**Rough numbers to remember:**
- A share of a large company like Amazon costs ~$284. A share of a cheaper company like SoFi costs ~$18. The price alone doesn't tell you if it's "cheap" — you need to compare to earnings per share (the P/E ratio).
- Market cap ranges: $3T (Amazon, Apple) is huge; $1B is a small company; $100B+ is a large company.

**Common misconception:** "A low stock price means it's cheap." Not necessarily. A $50 stock of a company with $0.10 per share in earnings is expensive (P/E of 500). A $500 stock of a company with $50 per share in earnings is cheap (P/E of 10). Always compare the price to the earnings.

### Concept Spotlight: Strait of Hormuz

**What it is:** The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow waterway between the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman. It's about 21 miles wide at its narrowest point — just wide enough for two large ships to pass in opposite directions. It connects the oil-rich Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, UAE, Qatar) to the open ocean.

**Why it matters:** About 20% of the world's seaborne oil passes through the Strait of Hormuz — roughly 17 million barrels per day. That's more than a fifth of all the oil traded globally. It's also a major route for liquefied natural gas (LNG) from Qatar.

**Who can close it and what happens if they try:** Iran's coastline borders the Strait. Iran has mined the Strait in the past (during the Iran-Iraq war in the 1980s) and has threatened to close it during conflicts. If Iran were to block the Strait:
- Oil prices would spike immediately — likely by 20-30% or more.
- Countries that depend on Gulf oil (Japan, China, India, South Korea) would face severe shortages.
- The US Navy would likely intervene to reopen the Strait, which could lead to open conflict.

**Why it's in the news today:** Trump announced he called off strikes on Iran, and talks may or may not be happening. The Strait is the subtext of every Iran-US standoff, because it's the chokepoint that makes Iran geopolitically important. Even if no strike happens, the threat of closure keeps oil prices elevated.

**Rough numbers:**
- 17 million barrels per day through the Strait.
- Alternative routes (pipelines) can bypass the Strait, but only for a fraction of the volume. The only major pipeline is the Saudi east-west pipeline, which can carry about 5 million barrels per day.
- A closure would remove about 12 million barrels per day from the global market, which is roughly 12% of global oil production.

**Common misconception:** "Iran can easily close the Strait." In practice, closing the Strait would require sustained military action, and the US Navy is powerful enough to keep it open. Iran's threat is more about disruption than permanent closure. Even a week of disruption would cause global economic damage.

### Terms from today's news

- **Context window:** The amount of text (in tokens) that a model can "see" at once. A 1-million-token context window means it can process about 750,000 words (roughly 3 novels) in one go. This is important for tasks like analyzing entire codebases or long legal documents.
- **Active parameters (in MoE):** In a Mixture-of-Experts model, only a fraction of the total parameters are used for each input. Qwen3.8-Max has 2.4 trillion total parameters but only 95 billion active — meaning it's 25x larger than GPT-3 in total but only about 3x larger in active compute. This keeps inference costs manageable.
- **Proof assistant (Lean):** A software tool that checks mathematical proofs for correctness. OpenAI's Astra used Lean to verify its math results, meaning the proofs are machine-checked — not just human-reviewed.
- **Disaggregated inference:** A technique where the "prefill" (processing the input) and "decode" (generating output) stages of an LLM run on separate GPU pools. This improves efficiency but creates a data transfer bottleneck (the KV cache must be moved between pools).
- **PFAS (forever chemicals):** A class of chemicals that don't break down in the environment. They're used in semiconductor manufacturing. The Micron lawsuit alleges PFAS contamination from the planned fab.
- **Grid interconnection:** The process of connecting a new power plant (or data center) to the electrical grid. It often takes years due to studies, permits, and upgrades. This is becoming a bottleneck for AI data centers.
- **PPA (Power Purchase Agreement):** A long-term contract to buy electricity from a specific generator. Hyperscalers sign PPAs with nuclear and renewable developers to secure power for data centers.
