## 5. Foundations & Terms

### Concept Spotlight: Circular Financing

**What it is in plain words:** Circular financing is when a company uses its own money (or money raised from investors) to build the infrastructure that its customers need, knowing that those customers will then spend that money back on the company's own products. It is a closed loop of investment and revenue.

**Why it exists:** In the AI industry, the biggest bottleneck is compute (GPUs and data centers). The companies that make the best AI models (like Anthropic, OpenAI) need enormous amounts of compute, but building data centers is incredibly expensive and takes years. The companies that make the compute (like Nvidia) have a strong incentive to help their customers build data centers, because every data center built is a guaranteed customer for Nvidia's chips.

**A concrete worked example with real numbers:**
1.  Nvidia has spent almost **$165 billion** funding AI data center projects (like the SB Energy campus in Ohio for OpenAI) ([O'Reilly Media](https://www.facebook.com/OReilly/videos/nvidia-has-spent-almost-165-billion-funding-the-ai-data-centers-that-run-its-chi/1115084801127768)).
2.  These data centers are built specifically to run Nvidia's GPUs.
3.  The data center operators (like CoreWeave, or the hyperscalers) buy Nvidia's GPUs to fill these data centers.
4.  Nvidia books the revenue from selling the GPUs.
5.  Nvidia uses that revenue (and its high stock price) to raise more money to invest in the *next* wave of data centers.

**How it shows up in the news today:** The Anthropic IPO is the next step in this cycle. Anthropic has huge compute commitments to Amazon and Google. By going public and raising $81B+, it gets the cash to pay for that compute. Amazon and Google, in turn, use that cash to buy more Nvidia GPUs. The IPO is the fuel for the circular financing engine.

**A figure worth remembering:** **$165 billion** -- the amount Nvidia has spent funding the data centers that buy its chips.

**A common misconception:** "It's a Ponzi scheme." It is not a Ponzi scheme because there is a real product (AI models and GPUs) being bought and sold. The risk is that it is a *virtuous cycle* that can become a *vicious cycle* if demand for AI models falls short of expectations. If the models aren't profitable enough, the money flowing into the loop will stop, leaving empty data centers and unsold GPUs.

**Other Terms from Today's News:**

- **Trilateral Summit:** A meeting between the leaders of three countries. This is rare for the US, China, and Russia, who are usually rivals. It signals a desire to manage competition directly, even if no major agreements are reached.
- **Flipping (in an IPO):** Selling shares of a newly public company within the first few days or weeks of its IPO to make a quick profit. Brokerages often discourage it because they want long-term holders. Source 118 reports that brokerages may penalize investors who flip Anthropic stock.
- **Family Office:** A private company that manages the investments and finances of a single very wealthy family (or a small group of wealthy individuals). The Anthropic IPO is expected to create dozens of new family offices to manage the sudden wealth of its employees ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/anthropic-ipo-ai-wealth-spawn-a-new-leaner-kind-of-family-office.html)).
- **Stock Split:** A corporate action where a company divides its existing shares into multiple new shares to make the stock price more affordable for retail investors. It does not change the company's total value. Sandisk is doing a stock split after today's close ([MarketWatch](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/sandisks-stock-split-is-almost-here-this-is-what-investors-need-to-know-e0a18461)).
