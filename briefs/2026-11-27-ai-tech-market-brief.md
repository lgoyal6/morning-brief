## 5. Foundations & Terms

### Concept Spotlight: Jevons Paradox

**What it is in plain words:** The idea that making something cheaper to use can lead to so much more of it being used that total spending on it goes up, not down. Named after 19th-century economist William Stanley Jevons, who noticed that more efficient coal engines led to more coal consumption, not less.

**Why it exists:** Demand is elastic. When the price of something useful drops, people find new ways to use it that were previously not worth the cost.

**Concrete worked example with real numbers:** In 2023, running a model like GPT-3 cost roughly $0.02 per 1,000 tokens. By 2026, models like DeepSeek-V3 and GigaChat 4 have pushed inference costs down by 90-95%. Developers didn't just use the same amount of AI for 10% of the cost. They built AI agents, coding assistants, and automated workflows that use 100x more tokens. Total token consumption exploded, but revenue per token collapsed.

**How it shows up in the news today:** The MarketWatch article (SOURCE 90) notes that AI usage is exploding, but spending is stalling. This is the Jevons Paradox in action for the AI industry.

**A figure worth remembering:** A 90% drop in cost can lead to a 1000x increase in usage.

**A common misconception:** That cheaper AI means less revenue for AI companies. In the long run, it usually means a much larger market, just with thinner margins per token. The winners are the companies that own the *platform* or the *application*, not just the raw compute.

**- Discrete Logarithm Problem:** A mathematical function that is easy to compute in one direction (exponentiation) but incredibly hard to reverse (finding the exponent). It is the foundation of most modern cryptography, including the security of Bitcoin, HTTPS, and Signal. A new paper claims a "deterministic subexponential-time algorithm" for solving it ([Hacker News](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/3020.pdf)). If this algorithm were practical and polynomial-time, it would break the internet's security. For now, it is a theoretical breakthrough, not a practical threat, but it underscores the urgency of moving to post-quantum cryptography.
