**Runway Praxis-1: Open-weights Robotics Model**

Runway released Praxis-1, a 27B parameter open-weights (Apache 2.0 license) diffusion-transformer world action model (WAM) for robotics. This lowers the barrier for physical AI and creates a viable open-source alternative to closed models for specific tasks.

**Builder's Swap: Runway Praxis-1 vs GPT-6 Astra**
- **Model:** Runway Praxis-1 (27B, Apache 2.0, open-weights)
- **Replaces:** GPT-6 Astra (closed, expensive cloud API) for robotics/world action tasks.
- **Capability gap:** Praxis-1 is specialized for robotics and world action modeling. It is not a general-purpose chatbot. It excels at its specific task but cannot replace GPT-6 for general coding or conversation.
- **Price:** Praxis-1 is free, self-hosted (needs a GPU with ~24GB+ VRAM). GPT-6 Astra is expensive per task (the source claims 51x higher cost).

Fed raises rates -> Cost of debt rises -> Hyperscalers face higher interest payments on their AI debt -> They slow down capex growth -> The AI buildout pace decelerates -> Nvidia and other AI hardware suppliers feel the pinch.

- **PRECEDENT:** The 2022 rate hike cycle crushed unprofitable tech stocks and forced a capex pullback at Meta (the "year of efficiency").

## 5. Foundations & Terms

### Concept Spotlight: Event Risk

**What it is:** The risk that a specific, unexpected event will cause a significant and sudden loss in value for an investment. It is the "tail risk" that the market did not price in.

**Why it exists:** Markets are good at pricing in a range of expected outcomes based on history and current data. An "event" is something outside that range — a surprise that forces everyone to re-evaluate the company or asset overnight.

**Concrete example with real numbers:** Jensen Huang's resignation is a textbook event risk for Nvidia. The stock fell sharply, and the broader AI trade was rattled (AMD down 8.75%). A board director cashed out roughly $1 billion in stock *before* the scandal broke ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/nvidia-board-director-cashed-out-usd1-billion-in-stock-before-jensen-huang-scandal-mark-stevens-sold-4-2-million-shares-without-a-trading-plan)). The DRAM price-fixing lawsuit against Micron, Samsung, and SK hynix is another example ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/big-tech/judge-denies-motion-to-dismiss-dram-price-fixing-lawsuit-against-micron-samsung-and-sk-hynix-says-chipmakers-acted-like-hawks-flying-in-formation-finds-evidence-of-dram-collusion-plausible-after-500-percent-price-surge)).

**How it shows up in the news:** Usually as a sudden price gap at the market open, a spike in the VIX (the "fear gauge"), a flurry of analyst downgrades, or a trading halt.

**A figure worth remembering:** A single event risk can wipe out months or years of gains. Nvidia lost hundreds of billions in market cap in the days following the scandal.

**A common misconception:** Event risk is the same as volatility. Volatility is the *measure* of price swings (how much and how fast the price moves). Event risk is the *cause* of a specific, large swing. You can have high volatility without a single event (e.g., a slow grind lower), and you can have an event without high volatility (e.g., a stock gaps down 10% and stays there).

**Other Terms from Today's News:**

- **World Action Model (WAM):** A type of AI model that understands the physical world and can generate actions (e.g., for a robot). Unlike a language model that just predicts the next word, a WAM predicts the next state of the world or the next action to take. Runway's Praxis-1 is an example.
- **Diffusion-Transformer:** A hybrid AI architecture. "Diffusion" is the process of adding noise to data and learning to reverse it (used in image/video generation like Stable Diffusion). "Transformer" is the architecture behind LLMs like GPT. Combining them allows the model to generate coherent sequences (like video or robot actions) by iteratively denoising them.
- **Abliteration:** (Still learning). The process of removing safety fine-tuning from an open-weight model to make it "uncensored". Relevant to the open-source AI debate.
