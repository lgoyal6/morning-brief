## 4. Model & Research Watch

**Step 5 Preview was fully released — China's cheapest frontier model yet.** With approximately 600 billion parameters (the "weights" or "knowledge" of the model, roughly analogous to the number of connections in a brain), Step 5 Preview matches "K3-level performance" (likely a Chinese benchmark ranking). The API pricing is stunningly aggressive: $1.00 per 1 million input tokens, $0.05 per 1 million cached input tokens, and $2.70 per 1 million output tokens ([Intelligent Living](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMieEFVX3lxTE04ZGszOENYYldzdGFIeWw0RUM4ck5NZnB5bHJaRzB4WHdneE5tRzR3amJ6VVJDN3pRVVl4NWFJVjExcWZWQUk1U09EVDV5YmMwT0VTRk56TkYxR20zMGR6SjU2SlVtT25FdEd2eVJRMlhDZ0ozOWVNQQ?oc=5)). For context, OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra costs significantly more. The model outputs about 100 tokens per second, faster than the average reasoning model. Step 5 Preview has closed weights (the company does not share the underlying model data, so you can only use it via their API), but the price point puts pressure on every other provider.

**PrismML released Ternary Bonsai 2 27B — a massive leap for local AI.** This model is an Apache 2.0 licensed (fully open, can be used for anything including commercial purposes) open-weight model that retains 98.2% of the performance of Qwen3.8 27B (a top-tier 27B parameter model) while being compressed to just 5.9 GB ([MarkTechPost](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMi3wFBVV95cUxOdkFtaUcyWHBsTERHU196dno3MTRpUWZTbU1pTTR3dklZbEgwSzIwNEEzZTY4NFF0UlFHYkVyOUhIN1UwN21fMXhoVGlIYlJaQVdfQ1BrWEhodVhLaUg5eUZ1U2tFNERraVVZR0FVM2lYdEhIOGt1OVE1N3JIaXBZUDBHRXRwVDgyN2YzMzJGOF9DQl9JeW02cll5TDg0VHB1a3BReG9mMXVaYVBia19majhpUDZWZDM0TXRZUlNKY3FkVl9lSE05dGEzYzdpSDREYVFOc0FBSWdZMWhoQjJV0gHkAUFVX3lxTE1aUy1PR3NpN205djEtaWlKUnVUOVF4RlRSSkFNM2ZWcVJfOGkwbW1LUnZ0MU5qNnBXRnlFVkFHem91cTE1aGZpMDBqRkFMekhZRzlNX1JVamtndzlzYWdEd0ljWm1NeEQ5YWpCNllpcVRxeldObHJiQ2sxbXBpZ0NZb2YxU1N2MnhZZnBGNlJfRmlNQldKSzhFaVpKSXFpTjZVYVZmMmNPOFhncnl4XzJZemF3c0JSa1BCeWx0cmpPbHJvMzhPSE4zWEg5T2UzZkg1d2E3QUpMc04xZElaZjhnX0daUA?oc=5)). This uses a technique called "ternary" quantization (quantization means reducing the precision of the model's numbers to make it smaller and faster; ternary is an extreme form where each number is only -1, 0, or 1). A 27B parameter model that fits in 5.9 GB and runs on a laptop is a genuinely big deal.

**Chain 3: Record diesel prices + tariffs + rising rates squeezing American companies**

## 9. Foundations & Terms

### Concept Spotlight: The Federal Funds Rate Hike Cycle

**What it is:** The federal funds rate is the interest rate at which banks lend money to each other overnight. When the Federal Reserve (the Fed) "raises rates," it pushes this rate higher. This is the most powerful tool the central bank has to control the economy.

**Why it exists / what problem it solves:** The Fed raises rates when the economy is growing too fast and inflation is too high. Higher rates make borrowing more expensive for everyone — mortgages, car loans, business loans, credit cards. When borrowing is expensive, people and companies spend less, which cools the economy and brings down prices.

**A concrete example:** Imagine a company that wants to borrow $1 billion to build a data center. If interest rates are 5%, they might pay $50 million a year in interest. If the Fed raises rates to 6%, the interest cost jumps to $60 million. That extra $10 million might be enough to make the project unprofitable, so they cancel it. That cancellation is the "cooling" effect the Fed wants.

**How it shows up in the news:** Today's Fed rate hike (Source 18) is the first since 2023. The market immediately repriced stocks lower because higher rates mean future profits are worth less today. The "dot plot" — the Fed's own projection of where rates are headed — shows that more hikes are coming.

**Rough benchmarks worth remembering:** A 1% (100 basis point) increase in rates typically reduces stock prices by roughly 10-15% on average, because it reduces the present value of future cash flows. Long-duration assets (high-growth tech stocks, like Nvidia or Tesla) are hit harder than short-duration assets (utilities, consumer staples).

**Caution or common misconception:** The fed funds rate does not directly set mortgage rates or credit card rates. It is the *target* for the overnight bank lending rate, but it ripples through the entire financial system. Also, the Fed has been wrong before — rate hikes in 2022 didn't immediately slow inflation because supply chains were still broken. The effect can take 12-18 months to fully hit the economy.

### Concept Spotlight: Proxy War and Escalation Dynamics

**What it is:** A proxy war is a conflict where two major powers use third parties (proxies) to fight each other instead of fighting directly. The major powers supply weapons, money, training, and intelligence to their side, but they avoid sending their own troops into direct combat.

**Why it exists / what problem it solves:** Direct war between nuclear-armed powers is incredibly dangerous. Using proxies allows a country to advance its strategic interests, weaken its adversary, and test new tactics without triggering a direct confrontation that could spiral into nuclear war.

**A concrete worked example:**
- **Iran's proxies:** Iran funds, arms, and trains the Houthis in Yemen and Hezbollah in Lebanon. Today, the Houthis attacked Riyadh with a missile (Source 123). Iran did not fire that missile; the Houthis did. But Iran achieved its goal of pressuring Saudi Arabia and disrupting US allies, without giving the US a direct legal or military justification to attack Iran.
- **The US's proxy:** The US supplies intelligence, weapons, and satellite data to Ukraine. Ukrainian drones hit Moscow today (Source 51). The US did not fire those drones. But the US achieves its goal of weakening Russia, without NATO troops fighting Russia.

**Rough benchmarks worth remembering:** Conflicts are usually fought by proxies when one or both of the major powers involved has nuclear weapons. The US-Soviet Cold War was fought almost entirely through proxies (Korea, Vietnam, Afghanistan). Today, the US-Iran conflict and the US-Russia conflict (via Ukraine) follow the same pattern.

**Caution or common misconception:**
- *Misconception:* "Proxies are just puppets." In reality, proxies have their own agendas. The Houthis want to rule Yemen; Iran uses them, but they are not fully controllable. Sometimes the proxy drags its patron into a wider war against the patron's wishes.
- *The risk:* Escalation can "slip its leash." A successful Houthi missile strike that kills hundreds of Americans in Saudi Arabia could force the US to retaliate directly against Iran, even if neither side wants a direct war. This is called an "escalation spiral" — each side does more, and the conflict grows beyond anyone's control.

### Other Terms from Today's News

- **Antitrust lawsuit:** A lawsuit brought under laws designed to prevent companies from colluding or forming monopolies that harm consumers. In today's news, four AI labs are accused of illegally agreeing to slow development. The goal of antitrust law is to protect competition.
- **Class action:** A lawsuit filed by one or more plaintiffs on behalf of a larger group ("class") of people who have similar claims. Today's AI lawsuit is a proposed class action.
- **Proxy (geopolitical context):** A third party used by a major power to fight or act on its behalf. The Houthis are a proxy for Iran.
- **Merger & Acquisition (M&A):** The process of one company buying another (acquisition) or two companies combining (merger). The Paramount-Warner Bros. deal is M&A. "M&A activity" is a measure of corporate confidence — when companies are bullish, they buy things.
- **All-cash deal:** A merger or acquisition where the buyer pays entirely in cash rather than stock. Williams's $5.5 billion purchase of Momentum Midstream appears to be an all-cash deal.
- **War-risk insurance premium:** The extra cost that shipping companies pay to insure their vessels when they are operating in a conflict zone. With the Strait of Hormuz closed, any ship that goes near it faces much higher premiums, which adds to global shipping costs.
- **Share buyback (or buyback):** When a company uses its own cash to buy its shares from the stock market. This reduces the number of shares outstanding, which increases the value of the remaining shares. Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway is famous for its buyback strategy.
